DHA exam for nurses (Registered Nurse, RNT0312) guide

Last checked against official documents: 17 November 2026

The Dubai Health Authority (DHA) Registered Nurse exam (code RNT0312) has 150 multiple-choice questions in 3 hours, and the pass mark is 50%. It costs USD 240, the result is pass or fail only, and you get 3 attempts at DHA. It covers the same four areas as the Saudi and Qatar nursing exams: Nursing Fundamentals, Adult Nursing, Maternal-Child Nursing, and Unit Management and Leadership. DHA publishes no weights for these areas.

Question 1

An older client on sedatives has not fallen but has several fall risk factors. Which type of nursing diagnosis applies?

  1. A medical diagnosis
  2. A health-promotion diagnosis
  3. A problem-focused diagnosis
  4. A risk diagnosis
Show answer

D. A risk diagnosis A risk nursing diagnosis, such as Risk for falls, describes a problem that has not yet occurred but for which risk factors are present. A problem-focused diagnosis describes a problem that is already happening, with defining characteristics. A health-promotion diagnosis reflects readiness to improve wellbeing. A medical diagnosis names a disease and is made by the provider.

Source: Open RN – Nursing Fundamentals 2e, 4.4 Diagnosis

Question 2

Amoxicillin 375 mg is prescribed. The suspension contains 250 mg in 5 mL. How many mL should the nurse give?

  1. 5 mL
  2. 6.5 mL
  3. 7.5 mL
  4. 10 mL
Show answer

C. 7.5 mL Use desired ÷ have × volume: 375 mg ÷ 250 mg × 5 mL = 1.5 × 5 = 7.5 mL. 5 mL would give only 250 mg. 10 mL would give 500 mg. 6.5 mL gives 325 mg, which is an underdose. Checking that the answer is between 5 and 10 mL confirms that it is reasonable.

Source: BCcampus – A Guide to Numeracy in Nursing (2023), Calculating Medication Dosage

Question 3

A nurse cannot obtain enough blood for a finger-stick glucose test from a client with cold hands. What should the nurse do?

  1. Squeeze the fingertip hard until blood flows
  2. Warm the hand and hold it below heart level
  3. Raise the hand above the head for a minute
  4. Use the first drop that smears on the skin
Show answer

B. Warm the hand and hold it below heart level Warming the hand and keeping it in a dependent position, below the level of the heart, causes vasodilation and improves blood flow for a good sample. A warm compress for about 10 minutes can also help. Squeezing the finger hard pushes tissue fluid into the sample and can give a false result. Raising the hand reduces blood flow, and a smeared drop is not a reliable sample.

Source: Open RN – Nursing Skills 2e, 19.2 Blood Glucose Monitoring

Question 4

A client with dysphagia is prescribed nectar-thick liquids. How should these liquids look?

  1. As thick as pudding, needing a spoon
  2. Thin and watery, like plain water
  3. Easily pourable, like apricot nectar
  4. Solid, like a mashed vegetable
Show answer

C. Easily pourable, like apricot nectar Thickened liquids are prescribed for dysphagia to reduce the risk of aspiration. Nectar-thick liquids are easily pourable and comparable to apricot nectar or thick cream soups. Honey-thick liquids are thicker, and pudding-thick or spoon-thick foods hold their shape. Thin liquids such as water are the most likely to be aspirated.

Source: Open RN – Nursing Fundamentals 2e, 14.3 Applying the Nursing Process (Nutrition)

Question 5

A client with borderline personality disorder says she will cut herself if her request is refused. What is the nurse's priority?

  1. Agree to her request to prevent harm
  2. Leave her alone to calm down by herself
  3. Ignore it, as it is attention-seeking
  4. Take it seriously; remove sharp items
Show answer

D. Take it seriously; remove sharp items Any threat of self-harm is taken seriously, even if the behaviour seems manipulative, and safety comes first: the nurse limits access to sharp objects or other means of injury and increases observation according to policy. Ignoring the threat or leaving her alone risks injury. Giving in to the demand reinforces manipulation and does not address safety.

Source: Open RN – Nursing: Mental Health and Community Concepts 2e, 10.4 Applying the Nursing Process to Personality Disorders

Question 6

A client with chronic generalised anxiety and a past substance use disorder needs long-term medication. Which drug is most appropriate?

  1. Lorazepam
  2. Alprazolam
  3. Diazepam
  4. Buspirone
Show answer

D. Buspirone Buspirone is a non-benzodiazepine medicine for chronic anxiety. Benzodiazepines such as lorazepam, alprazolam and diazepam can cause misuse and physical dependence, and stopping them suddenly can cause dangerous withdrawal, so they are less suitable for long-term use, especially with a history of substance misuse.

Source: Open RN – Nursing: Mental Health and Community Concepts 2e, 6.5 Antianxiety Medications

Question 7

What builds up inside artery walls to form plaque in atherosclerosis?

  1. Uric acid crystals
  2. Calcium from the bones only
  3. Red blood cell clots
  4. Cholesterol and LDL deposits
Show answer

D. Cholesterol and LDL deposits Atherosclerosis is the build-up of cholesterol, low-density lipoprotein (LDL) deposits and waste within the lining of the arteries, forming plaque that narrows the vessels and causes inflammation. Diabetes and high blood glucose add to the process. Uric acid crystals cause gout, and clots can form on plaque but are not the plaque itself.

Source: Open RN – Health Alterations, 5.6 Arteriosclerosis & Atherosclerosis

Question 8

A client with asthma is prescribed propranolol for migraine prevention. Why should the nurse question the order?

  1. It must not be taken with food
  2. It causes severe hypertension
  3. It can trigger bronchospasm
  4. It always causes tachycardia
Show answer

C. It can trigger bronchospasm Propranolol is a non-selective beta-blocker that blocks both beta-1 and beta-2 receptors. Blocking beta-2 receptors in the airways can cause bronchospasm, so it is used cautiously, if at all, in clients with asthma or COPD. It lowers blood pressure and heart rate rather than raising them, and it can also mask signs of hypoglycaemia.

Source: Open RN – Nursing Pharmacology 2e, 4.14 Beta-2 Antagonists

Question 9

During a central venous catheter dressing change, the nurse scrubs the site with antiseptic. What should the nurse do next?

  1. Wipe it dry with sterile gauze
  2. Blow gently on the site to dry it
  3. Fan the site with a sterile drape
  4. Let it dry naturally without fanning
Show answer

D. Let it dry naturally without fanning After cleansing, the antiseptic must be allowed to dry naturally for the time the manufacturer recommends. Wiping, fanning or blowing on the skin removes the product before it has worked and can add contamination. Applying the new dressing only once the site is dry lowers the risk of a central line infection.

Source: Open RN – Nursing Advanced Skills, 4.4 Checklist: Change a CVAD Dressing and Needleless Connector

Question 10

What does it mean when a drug is described as an agonist?

  1. It binds to a receptor and blocks its effect
  2. It speeds the drug's removal by the kidneys
  3. It binds to a receptor and produces an effect
  4. It is broken down by the liver before acting
Show answer

C. It binds to a receptor and produces an effect An agonist binds tightly to a receptor and produces the desired effect, copying the action of the body's own chemical. For example, morphine binds to opioid receptors to reduce pain. An antagonist binds to the receptor but blocks it, as naloxone does to reverse opioids. Excretion and liver breakdown are pharmacokinetic processes.

Source: Open RN – Nursing Pharmacology 2e, 1.7 Pharmacodynamics

Question 11

After the baby's head is born, the shoulders do not deliver with normal traction. Which manoeuvre is usually tried first?

  1. Pushing the head back in
  2. McRoberts manoeuvre
  3. Fundal pressure
  4. Leopold's manoeuvres
Show answer

B. McRoberts manoeuvre This is shoulder dystocia, an emergency with no reliable warning signs. The first manoeuvre is McRoberts: the woman's legs are flexed sharply so her thighs press against her abdomen, which widens the pelvic outlet. Fundal pressure can worsen impaction and is avoided. Leopold's manoeuvres are abdominal palpations done in pregnancy, not a delivery manoeuvre. Pushing the head back is a last-resort measure.

Source: Open RN – Nursing: Health Promotion, 10.8 Complications and Medical Interventions During the Second Stage of Labor

Question 12

A child in Dubai had the first MMR dose at 12 months. Under the UAE national immunisation schedule, when is the second dose of MMR given?

  1. 15 months
  2. 18 months
  3. 4–6 years
  4. 5–6 years
Show answer

B. 18 months The UAE national schedule gives the second MMR dose at 18 months. The 5–6 year visit gives DTaP–IPV, the second OPV booster and the second varicella dose, but not MMR, so a nurse should not wait until school age for the second MMR. 4–6 years is the US timing, and 15 months is not a routine visit.

Source: Dubai Health Authority – Guidelines for Best Practice in Immunization (DHA/PHPD/CG-04, Issue 4, effective 27 Jan 2026), Appendix 1: National Immunization Schedule – UAE

Question 13

Under the UAE national immunisation schedule followed in Dubai, when are the two doses of chickenpox (varicella) vaccine given?

  1. 12 months and 18 months
  2. 9 months and 18 months
  3. 12 months and 5–6 years
  4. 18 months and 4–6 years
Show answer

C. 12 months and 5–6 years In the UAE schedule, the first varicella dose is given at 12 months with the first MMR, and the second varicella dose at 5–6 years. This differs from MMR, whose second dose is at 18 months, so pairing varicella with both MMR visits is a common mistake. Starting at 18 months follows another country's schedule, and varicella is not given routinely at 9 months.

Source: Dubai Health Authority – Guidelines for Best Practice in Immunization (DHA/PHPD/CG-04, Issue 4, effective 27 Jan 2026), Appendix 1: National Immunization Schedule – UAE

Question 14

Which instruction should the nurse give the parents of a child after tonsillectomy?

  1. Encourage frequent nose blowing
  2. Do not let the child drink through a straw
  3. Encourage the child to clear the throat often
  4. Give hot drinks to soothe the throat
Show answer

B. Do not let the child drink through a straw Straws and sharp objects such as forks can damage the surgical site and cause bleeding, so they are avoided. Throat clearing, coughing and nose blowing are discouraged for the same reason. Cool, soft foods and fluids are preferred to hot drinks, which can irritate the throat.

Source: Open RN – Nursing: Health Promotion, 15.7 Pharyngitis, Tonsillitis, & Adenoiditis

Question 15

A new nurse realises she is wrong in a disagreement and gives in to her experienced colleague's plan. Which conflict resolution approach is this?

  1. Avoiding
  2. Accommodating
  3. Compromising
  4. Competing
Show answer

B. Accommodating In accommodating, one party resolves the conflict by giving in to the other. It can be useful when a person realises they are in error or the other party has more expertise or experience. Avoiding ignores the conflict, competing pursues one's own goal at the other's expense, and compromising has each side give up part of what they want.

Source: Open RN – Health Alterations, 1.5 Conflict Resolution

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are in the DHA nurse exam, and how long is it?

150 multiple-choice questions. Time: 3 hours, including registration and the introduction.

What is the DHA nurse exam pass mark?

The pass mark is 50%, but DHA reports the result only as pass or fail, without a score.

What topics does the DHA nurse exam cover?

Four areas: Nursing Fundamentals, Adult Nursing, Maternal-Child Nursing, and Unit Management and Leadership. They match the Saudi SNLE and the Qatar nursing exam, where Adult Nursing is about 40% and Maternal-Child Nursing about 30%.

How many times can I take the DHA nursing exam?

3 times at DHA, with no set waiting time between attempts. After a third fail, DHA blocks a new application for 2 years. You may be granted 1 final attempt with a different UAE authority, such as DOH Abu Dhabi or MOHAP.
