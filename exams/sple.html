SPLE: Saudi Pharmacist Licensure Exam guide

Last checked against official documents: 17 November 2026

The SPLE is the Saudi Commission for Health Specialties (SCFHS) licensing exam for pharmacists. It has 200 multiple-choice questions in two parts of 100, with 120 minutes for each part. The pass mark is a scaled score of 536 on a 200–800 scale, and you can sit it up to 4 times a year until you pass. Clinical Sciences and Pharmaceutical Sciences are the largest domains (35% each), followed by Social, Behavioral and Administrative Sciences (20%), which includes pharmacy law and regulatory affairs, and Basic Biomedical Sciences (10%).

Question 1

A woman has taken pyridoxine (vitamin B6) 500 mg daily for months for premenstrual symptoms. She now has numbness in her feet and an unsteady gait. What is the most likely cause?

  1. Vitamin B12 toxicity from the supplement
  2. Vitamin B6 deficiency from poor absorption
  3. Sensory neuropathy from vitamin B6 toxicity
  4. Hypercalcaemia caused by the supplement
Show answer

C. Sensory neuropathy from vitamin B6 toxicity Although vitamin B6 is water-soluble, chronic high-dose supplements can cause severe, progressive sensory neuropathy with numbness, ataxia and loss of position sense. Severity is dose-dependent, and cases have occurred below 500 mg/day. Symptoms usually improve after stopping the supplement. The adult upper limit is 100 mg/day. Low-dose pyridoxine is used to prevent isoniazid neuropathy.

Source: NIH Office of Dietary Supplements – Vitamin B6 Fact Sheet for Health Professionals

Question 2

A 30-year-old man develops cyanosis and chocolate-brown blood after heavy use of a topical benzocaine spray. His methaemoglobin level is 35%. Which treatment is indicated?

  1. Hydroxocobalamin
  2. Sodium thiosulfate
  3. Naloxone
  4. Methylene blue
Show answer

D. Methylene blue Benzocaine can oxidise haemoglobin to methaemoglobin, which cannot carry oxygen and causes cyanosis with chocolate-brown blood. Methylene blue is the antidote for acquired methaemoglobinaemia. It speeds the reduction of methaemoglobin back to haemoglobin. Hydroxocobalamin and sodium thiosulfate treat cyanide poisoning. Naloxone reverses opioids. Methylene blue should be avoided with serotonergic drugs because of serotonin syndrome risk.

Source: DailyMed – ProvayBlue (methylene blue) injection (label)

Question 3

A 70-kg adult needs IV acetylcysteine for paracetamol overdose using the three-bag regimen. What loading dose should be infused over the first hour?

  1. 3,500 mg
  2. 7,000 mg
  3. 10,500 mg
  4. 21,000 mg
Show answer

C. 10,500 mg The IV acetylcysteine loading dose is 150 mg/kg over 1 hour: 150 × 70 = 10,500 mg. It is followed by 50 mg/kg over 4 hours and 100 mg/kg over 16 hours. 3,500 mg is the second-bag dose (50 mg/kg). 7,000 mg is the third-bag dose (100 mg/kg). 21,000 mg is the total over all three bags (300 mg/kg).

Source: DailyMed – Acetadote (acetylcysteine) injection (label, Table 2)

Question 4

A pharmacist is preparing liposomal amphotericin B for injection. Which diluent should be used to reconstitute the vial?

  1. Sodium chloride 0.9%
  2. Bacteriostatic water for injection
  3. Lactated Ringer's solution
  4. Sterile water for injection
Show answer

D. Sterile water for injection Amphotericin B products are reconstituted with sterile water for injection without a bacteriostatic agent, then diluted in 5% dextrose. Saline and other electrolyte-containing solutions can make amphotericin precipitate. Bacteriostatic water contains preservatives that may also cause precipitation. Lactated Ringer's contains electrolytes, so it is equally unsuitable.

Source: DailyMed – Amphotericin B (liposome) for injection (label, Dosage and Administration)

Question 5

Which sterilisation method is the most widely used and most dependable for heat- and moisture-stable items such as surgical instruments?

  1. Dry heat sterilisation in an oven
  2. Ethylene oxide gas
  3. Saturated steam under pressure
  4. Ionising gamma radiation
Show answer

C. Saturated steam under pressure Moist heat as saturated steam under pressure (autoclaving) is the most widely used and dependable sterilisation method. It is fast, non-toxic, cheap and kills spores. Dry heat needs higher temperatures and longer times. Ethylene oxide is used for heat-sensitive items but is toxic and slow. Radiation is used mainly by industry for disposables.

Source: CDC – Guideline for Disinfection and Sterilization in Healthcare Facilities: Steam Sterilization

Question 6

Irinotecan, used for metastatic colorectal cancer, acts by inhibiting which enzyme?

  1. Topoisomerase II
  2. Topoisomerase I
  3. Thymidylate synthase
  4. Dihydrofolate reductase
Show answer

B. Topoisomerase I Irinotecan and its active metabolite SN-38 bind the topoisomerase I-DNA complex and prevent religation of single-strand breaks, leading to cell death. Etoposide and anthracyclines inhibit topoisomerase II. Fluorouracil inhibits thymidylate synthase, and methotrexate inhibits dihydrofolate reductase. Late diarrhoea is a major irinotecan toxicity and is treated promptly with loperamide.

Source: DailyMed – Irinotecan Hydrochloride Injection (label, 12.1)

Question 7

A Saudi hospital is preparing for its national accreditation survey. Which body accredits healthcare facilities in Saudi Arabia?

  1. Saudi Food and Drug Authority (SFDA)
  2. Saudi Central Board for Accreditation (CBAHI)
  3. Saudi Commission for Health Specialties
  4. World Health Organization (WHO)
Show answer

B. Saudi Central Board for Accreditation (CBAHI) CBAHI (Saudi Central Board for Accreditation of Healthcare Institutions) is the national body for accrediting healthcare facilities in Saudi Arabia. The SFDA regulates medicines, food and devices. The SCFHS registers and licenses healthcare practitioners and runs licensing exams such as the SPLE. The WHO issues international guidance but does not accredit Saudi hospitals.

Source: CBAHI – official portal

Question 8

A married adult woman arrives with a life-threatening ectopic pregnancy and consents to emergency surgery. Her husband cannot be reached. According to the Saudi Code of Ethics for Healthcare Practitioners, whose consent is required?

  1. Her own consent is sufficient
  2. Her husband's consent is required
  3. Her father's consent is required
  4. A court order must be obtained
Show answer

A. Her own consent is sufficient The SCFHS Code states that in emergency and life-threatening conditions, the woman's consent is sufficient. A competent adult woman can consent to her own treatment, and life-saving care must not be delayed while waiting for a husband or guardian. Where a patient lacks capacity and no guardian can be reached, the practitioner may act to prevent loss of life or serious harm. Consent should be documented in the record.

Source: SCFHS – Code of Ethics for Healthcare Practitioners (English, 2026), Chapter 2

Question 9

A clinical pharmacist from another ward wants to review a patient's full file out of curiosity, without being asked by the treating team. Is this acceptable?

  1. Acceptable if he does not change any of the treatment
  2. Acceptable, since all hospital staff may read any patient file
  3. Not acceptable unless the treating team involves him in care
  4. Acceptable as long as the patient is never told about it
Show answer

C. Not acceptable unless the treating team involves him in care The SCFHS Code states that it is not acceptable to access a patient's file without the permission of the treating healthcare practitioner, and that consultants should respond when the treating practitioner requests a consultation. This protects confidentiality and the patient–practitioner relationship. Access to records should be limited to those directly involved in care, and electronic access is often audited. Curiosity is never a valid reason, so access depends on being asked to help by the treating team.

Source: SCFHS – Code of Ethics for Healthcare Practitioners (English, 2026), Chapter 4

Question 10

A 27-year-old woman at 8 weeks' gestation is newly diagnosed with Graves' hyperthyroidism and needs an antithyroid drug. Which agent is preferred at this stage of pregnancy?

  1. Methimazole
  2. Propylthiouracil
  3. Carbimazole
  4. Radioactive iodine
Show answer

B. Propylthiouracil Propylthiouracil is preferred during and just before the first trimester. Methimazole and its prodrug carbimazole are linked to birth defects when taken during early organ development. PTU carries a risk of severe liver injury, so patients are usually switched to methimazole after the first trimester. Radioactive iodine is contraindicated in pregnancy because it destroys the fetal thyroid.

Source: DailyMed – Propylthiouracil Tablets (label)

Question 11

A 78-year-old woman with a creatinine clearance of 22 mL/min has an uncomplicated lower urinary tract infection. Her prescriber asks whether nitrofurantoin capsules can be used. What is the correct advice?

  1. Avoid it, as her renal function is too poor for it
  2. Give half the usual dose for 7 days to allow for her CrCl
  3. Give the usual dose for 5 days, as her CrCl is adequate
  4. Give the usual dose but extend the course to 14 days
Show answer

A. Avoid it, as her renal function is too poor for it Nitrofurantoin should be avoided at this level of renal function. The product labelling contraindicates it when creatinine clearance is under 60 mL/min, and current geriatric prescribing guidance still advises avoiding it below 30 mL/min. With poor renal function, urine levels are too low to work and systemic toxicity (e.g. neuropathy) rises. Lowering the dose or extending the course does not fix either problem.

Source: DailyMed – Macrobid (nitrofurantoin monohydrate/macrocrystals) capsules (label)

Question 12

A 62-year-old woman is starting ibandronate 150 mg once a month for osteoporosis. Which counselling point is correct?

  1. Take it with breakfast and your calcium tablet
  2. Take it with orange juice to improve its absorption
  3. Take it 60 minutes before food and stay upright for 60
  4. Take it at bedtime with a glass of warm milk
Show answer

C. Take it 60 minutes before food and stay upright for 60 Ibandronate is poorly absorbed and irritates the oesophagus. The monthly tablet is taken on the same date each month with plain water, at least 60 minutes before the first food, drink (other than water) or medicine of the day. The patient must not lie down for 60 minutes afterwards. Juice, milk, food and calcium all reduce absorption, and lying down at bedtime raises oesophageal risk.

Source: DailyMed – Boniva (ibandronate) tablets (label, 2.2)

Question 13

A 45-year-old opioid-naive man needs an opioid for severe pain after a fracture. According to CDC guidance, which is preferred when starting?

  1. Immediate-release opioid at the lowest effective dose
  2. Extended-release opioid for steady 24-hour levels
  3. Transdermal fentanyl patch for continuous pain relief
  4. Methadone because of its long half-life
Show answer

A. Immediate-release opioid at the lowest effective dose The 2022 CDC Clinical Practice Guideline advises that when opioids are started, clinicians should prescribe immediate-release opioids at the lowest effective dose instead of extended-release or long-acting opioids. Acute pain usually needs only a few days of treatment. ER/LA opioids and fentanyl patches carry higher overdose risk and are not for opioid-naive patients. Methadone accumulates unpredictably and is unsuitable for acute pain.

Source: CDC – Clinical Practice Guideline for Prescribing Opioids for Pain, 2022 (MMWR Recomm Rep 71(3))

Question 14

A patient takes clopidogrel for stable coronary disease (no recent stent) and is booked for elective hip replacement with a major bleeding risk. When should clopidogrel be stopped?

  1. 1 day before surgery
  2. 5 days before surgery
  3. 14 days before surgery
  4. It should not be stopped
Show answer

B. 5 days before surgery Clopidogrel irreversibly inhibits the platelet P2Y12 receptor, so its effect lasts for the life of the platelet. For elective surgery with a major bleeding risk, it should be stopped 5 days before, allowing enough new platelets to form. One day is too short, and 14 days unnecessarily raises thrombotic risk. In patients with recent stents, timing must be agreed with the cardiologist.

Source: DailyMed – Clopidogrel Tablets (label, Highlights 2.3)

Question 15

A 10-year-old with confirmed streptococcal pharyngitis started amoxicillin and feels much better on day 3. What is the recommended total duration of treatment?

  1. 3 days
  2. 5 days
  3. 7 days
  4. 10 days
Show answer

D. 10 days For group A streptococcal pharyngitis, amoxicillin or penicillin remains first-line, and CDC states the recommended course for all oral beta-lactams is 10 days. The full course is needed to eradicate the organism and prevent complications such as acute rheumatic fever, even after symptoms improve. Shorter courses increase the risk of relapse.

Source: CDC – Pediatric Outpatient Treatment Recommendations (Antibiotic Use)

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are in the SPLE, and how long is it?

200 multiple-choice questions, in 2 parts of 100. Time: 120 minutes for each part, with a 30-minute break between them.

What is the SPLE pass mark?

The pass mark is a scaled score of 536 on a 200–800 scale. It is not a simple percentage of correct answers.

What topics does the SPLE cover?

Clinical Sciences 35%, Pharmaceutical Sciences 35%, Social, Behavioral and Administrative Sciences 20% (including pharmacy law and regulatory affairs), and Basic Biomedical Sciences 10%.

Does the SPLE include pharmacy law?

Yes. "Pharmacy Law and Regulatory Affairs" is part of the Social, Behavioral and Administrative Sciences domain, which is 20% of the exam. The same domain covers Saudi healthcare organisations, and the guide's references include Saudi drug-control material.
