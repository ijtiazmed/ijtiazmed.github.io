Saudi physiotherapy licensing exam (SCFHS) guide

Last checked against official documents: 17 November 2026

The Saudi Commission for Health Specialties (SCFHS) physiotherapy specialist exam has 150 multiple-choice questions in 3 hours, and the pass mark is 60%. Its blueprint has 10 domains. Anatomy and Physiology, Special Populations, Neurorehabilitation and Musculoskeletal Rehabilitation carry 12% each. Physical Agents and Therapeutic Exercises carry 10% each, and the other four domains 8% each.

Question 1

A therapist measures knee flexion and extension with a goniometer. In which anatomical plane do these movements occur?

  1. Oblique plane
  2. Sagittal plane
  3. Frontal (coronal) plane
  4. Transverse (horizontal) plane
Show answer

B. Sagittal plane Flexion and extension occur in the sagittal plane, which divides the body into right and left parts, around a medial-lateral axis. The frontal (coronal) plane contains abduction and adduction. The transverse (horizontal) plane contains medial and lateral rotation. An oblique plane is a diagonal section and is not used to describe these standard joint movements.

Source: OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology (LibreTexts, CC BY) 9.5 Types of Body Movements

Question 2

In gait analysis, what defines one complete gait cycle (one stride)?

  1. From contact of one foot to contact of the other foot
  2. From initial contact of one foot to its next contact
  3. From toe-off of one foot to heel strike of the same foot
  4. From mid-stance to terminal stance of one limb
Show answer

B. From initial contact of one foot to its next contact A gait cycle, or stride, begins when one foot contacts the ground and ends when the same foot contacts the ground again, so it includes one stance and one swing phase of that limb (about 60% and 40%). The interval from contact of one foot to contact of the opposite foot is a step, half a stride. Toe-off to heel strike of the same foot is only the swing phase, and mid-stance to terminal stance is part of stance.

Source: University of Oklahoma HSC (D. Thompson): Conventions for naming parts of the gait cycle

Question 3

A 30-year-old man had a tight below-knee cast for a proximal fibula fracture. After removal he cannot lift his foot during swing and has numbness over the top of the foot and the lateral lower leg. Which nerve is most likely injured?

  1. Common fibular (peroneal) nerve
  2. Tibial nerve in the popliteal fossa
  3. Femoral nerve in the thigh
  4. Obturator nerve in the thigh
Show answer

A. Common fibular (peroneal) nerve The common fibular (peroneal) nerve, a branch of the sciatic nerve, wraps around the fibular neck, where a fracture or a tight cast can compress it. Damage causes foot drop (its deep branch supplies the ankle dorsiflexors) and numbness over the top of the foot and lateral leg. The tibial nerve supplies the plantar flexors and the sole. The femoral nerve supplies the quadriceps. The obturator nerve supplies the hip adductors.

Source: MedlinePlus Medical Encyclopedia: Common peroneal nerve dysfunction

Question 4

An 82-year-old has a red, warm, tender area on her lower leg that is spreading, with a mild fever and chills. She has cracked skin between her toes. Which condition is most likely?

  1. Cellulitis
  2. Peripheral arterial disease
  3. Varicose veins
  4. Chronic lymphoedema
Show answer

A. Cellulitis Cellulitis is a common bacterial skin infection that often enters through cracks between the toes or other wounds. It causes spreading redness, warmth, tenderness and swelling, often with fever and chills, and needs prompt medical treatment with antibiotics. Chronic lymphoedema causes painless swelling without fever, peripheral arterial disease causes cool, pale skin, and varicose veins are dilated superficial veins without infection.

Source: MedlinePlus Medical Encyclopedia: Cellulitis

Question 5

A 22-year-old footballer is 2 weeks after ACL reconstruction and has poor voluntary quadriceps contraction. Which adjunct to his strengthening program has the strongest evidence for improving quadriceps strength?

  1. Continuous therapeutic ultrasound over the patellar tendon
  2. Neuromuscular electrical stimulation for 6–8 weeks
  3. Infrared heating of the anterior thigh before exercise
  4. Interferential current to the knee for pain only
Show answer

B. Neuromuscular electrical stimulation for 6–8 weeks After ACL reconstruction, the knee guideline gives a grade A recommendation for neuromuscular electrical stimulation added to strengthening exercise for 6–8 weeks, to increase quadriceps strength and improve short-term function. Ultrasound does not build strength. Interferential current mainly modulates pain and does not produce strong contractions. Infrared only heats the skin and does not improve muscle activation.

Source: JOSPT CPG: Knee Ligament Sprain, revision (Logerstedt et al.), 2017

Question 6

At about what water temperature are silica-gel hot packs usually kept in a hydrocollator unit?

  1. 50 to 55 °C
  2. 90 to 95 °C
  3. 40 to 45 °C
  4. 70 to 75 °C
Show answer

D. 70 to 75 °C Silica-gel hot packs are stored in hydrocollator water at about 70 to 75 °C (158 to 167 °F). Because this is far hotter than the skin can tolerate, the pack is wrapped in 6 to 8 layers of towelling and applied for 15 to 20 minutes, with skin checks. Water at 40 to 55 °C would not keep the pack hot enough, and 90 to 95 °C would make burns likely.

Source: Lane Community College PTA course notes: Heat and Cold (PTA 101)

Question 7

A 30-year-old woman who was diagnosed with multiple sclerosis last month has mild balance problems and reports that fatigue limits her day. Which recommendation is most appropriate?

  1. Daily mild-to-moderate physical activity
  2. Delay all exercise until her symptoms progress
  3. High-intensity interval training in a warm studio
  4. Rest in bed on days when fatigue is present
Show answer

A. Daily mild-to-moderate physical activity Regular physical activity at mild to moderate intensity reduces fatigue in multiple sclerosis and helps maintain strength, balance and mobility. Referring early prevents problems caused by inactivity. Bed rest worsens deconditioning and fatigue. High-intensity work in a warm room can overheat her and temporarily worsen symptoms. Delaying exercise lets the effects of inactivity build up.

Source: NINDS: Multiple Sclerosis, 2024

Question 8

A 78-year-old woman with osteoporosis and a history of falls starts standing balance exercises at home. Which set-up is safest at first?

  1. Standing on one leg with eyes closed
  2. Standing on a foam pad in the middle of the room
  3. Standing in a corner with a sturdy chair in front
  4. Walking backwards with no support
Show answer

C. Standing in a corner with a sturdy chair in front In osteoporosis a fall can cause a fracture, so early balance exercises are done with firm upper-limb support within reach, such as standing in a corner or at a kitchen counter with a sturdy chair in front, and are progressed gradually. Foam surfaces, eyes closed, single-leg stance and backward walking without support are harder progressions that are added only once the person is safe and, at first, supervised.

Source: Lane Community College PTA course notes: Skeletal Demineralization (PTA 104)

Question 9

A 30-year-old man with paraplegia now spends most of the day in a manual wheelchair. How often should he shift his weight (leaning forward and side to side) to protect his skin?

  1. Every 15 to 20 minutes
  2. Every 4 hours
  3. Twice a day
  4. Only when he feels discomfort
Show answer

A. Every 15 to 20 minutes People who sit in a wheelchair for long periods should shift their weight (leaning forward, left and right) every 15 to 20 minutes to relieve pressure over the sitting bones. Waiting 4 hours or shifting twice a day allows prolonged pressure that can quickly cause ulcers. People with spinal cord injury often cannot feel discomfort, so relying on sensation is unsafe.

Source: MedlinePlus Patient Instructions: Preventing pressure ulcers

Question 10

A 79-year-old has age-related hearing loss (presbycusis). How should the physiotherapist explain his home exercises?

  1. Talk while the gym music is playing
  2. Face him and speak clearly, a bit louder
  3. Speak quickly so he keeps attention
  4. Shout the instructions close to his ear
Show answer

B. Face him and speak clearly, a bit louder People with presbycusis understand speech better when the speaker faces them, so expressions and lip movements can be seen, and speaks clearly and a little louder without shouting. Background noise such as music or a television should be turned down. Shouting distorts speech, competing noise makes understanding harder, and speaking quickly reduces comprehension. Written instructions are a useful addition.

Source: NIDCD: Age-Related Hearing Loss (Presbycusis)

Question 11

A 50-year-old man in an exercise program has repeated resting blood pressure readings of 134/86 mmHg and takes no medication. Using the ACC/AHA classification, how is this classified?

  1. Elevated systolic with normal diastolic
  2. High blood pressure (130/80 or higher)
  3. Normal blood pressure
  4. Low blood pressure
Show answer

B. High blood pressure (130/80 or higher) High blood pressure (hypertension) is defined as readings of 130/80 mmHg or higher most of the time, and normal blood pressure is below 120/80 mmHg. Readings of 134/86 mmHg therefore count as high blood pressure under this classification. They are neither normal nor low, and the diastolic value of 86 mmHg is itself above 80. Some other guidelines (for example ESC and WHO) use 140/90 mmHg as the threshold, which is why the classification is named.

Source: MedlinePlus Medical Encyclopedia: High blood pressure – adults

Question 12

A volleyball team physiotherapist wants to reduce first-time lateral ankle sprains in players with risk factors. Which strategy does the ankle guideline recommend (grade A)?

  1. Static calf stretching before matches only
  2. Avoiding all jump training
  3. Prophylactic ankle bracing
  4. Ultrasound to the ankle before games
Show answer

C. Prophylactic ankle bracing The 2021 ankle sprain guideline gives a grade A recommendation for prophylactic bracing to reduce the risk of a first-time lateral ankle sprain, particularly in athletes with risk factors. Stretching alone has not been shown to prevent ankle sprains. Avoiding all jump training is unrealistic in volleyball and does not build protective capacity. Ultrasound has no preventive role.

Source: JOSPT CPG: Lateral Ankle Ligament Sprains, revision (Martin et al.), 2021

Question 13

A club golfer has pain on the inside of the elbow that worsens when he grips the club, flexes the wrist or shakes hands. What is the most likely diagnosis?

  1. Lateral epicondylitis
  2. Olecranon bursitis
  3. Medial epicondylitis
  4. Distal biceps rupture
Show answer

C. Medial epicondylitis Medial epicondylitis (golfer's elbow) affects the common flexor-pronator origin on the medial epicondyle. It causes pain on the inside of the elbow and forearm that worsens with gripping, wrist flexion and handshakes, and is linked to golf, throwing, racquet sports and weight training. Lateral epicondylitis causes lateral pain with wrist extension, olecranon bursitis is a swelling at the back of the elbow, and a biceps rupture causes anterior pain and weakness.

Source: MedlinePlus Medical Encyclopedia: Medial epicondylitis – golfer's elbow

Question 14

A 71-year-old man with Parkinson's disease often freezes when starting to walk and when passing through doorways. Which intervention has strong evidence for reducing his freezing of gait?

  1. Prolonged static stretching of the hamstrings
  2. Avoiding walking until his medication is changed
  3. External cueing, such as a metronome beat
  4. Wearing a soft cervical collar during walking
Show answer

C. External cueing, such as a metronome beat The Parkinson disease guideline strongly recommends external cueing to reduce motor disease severity and freezing of gait. Examples are auditory cues such as a metronome or rhythmic counting, and visual cues such as lines on the floor. Hamstring stretching does not address the motor-planning problem behind freezing. A cervical collar has no role. Avoiding walking leads to deconditioning and does not reduce freezing.

Source: APTA CPG: Physical Therapist Management of Parkinson Disease (Osborne et al.), Phys Ther 2022

Question 15

Which joint distribution is most typical of rheumatoid arthritis?

  1. The same joints on both sides of the body
  2. Only the distal interphalangeal joints
  3. A single large joint on one side only
  4. Only the lumbar spine
Show answer

A. The same joints on both sides of the body Rheumatoid arthritis usually affects joints on both sides of the body equally (symmetrically), commonly the fingers, wrists, knees, feet, elbows and ankles. It is an autoimmune disease that can also affect other organs. A single large joint on one side suggests gout, septic arthritis or osteoarthritis. Isolated DIP involvement suggests osteoarthritis or psoriatic arthritis. Isolated lumbar involvement is not typical of rheumatoid arthritis.

Source: MedlinePlus Medical Encyclopedia: Rheumatoid arthritis

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are in the Saudi physiotherapy exam, and how long is it?

150 multiple-choice questions. Time: 3 hours.

What is the Saudi physiotherapy exam pass mark?

The pass mark is 60% of the questions, for tests from 21 June 2022 onwards, per the SCFHS Physiotherapy Specialist blueprint. Unlike the SNLE and SPLE, it is a percentage, not a scaled score.

What topics does the Saudi physiotherapy exam cover?

Ten domains: Anatomy and Physiology, Special Populations, Neurorehabilitation and Musculoskeletal Rehabilitation (12% each); Physical Agents and Therapeutic Exercises (10% each); and Kinesiology, Clinical Decision Making, Cardiopulmonary and Sports Physical Therapy (8% each).

Is the Saudi physiotherapy blueprint the same as Qatar's?

Yes. Qatar's DHP physiotherapist blueprint uses the same 10 domains with the same weights, so the same preparation covers both.
