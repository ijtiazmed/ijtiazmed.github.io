DHA exam for physiotherapists (PHY5121) guide

Last checked against official documents: 17 November 2026

The Dubai Health Authority (DHA) Physiotherapist exam (code PHY5121) has 150 multiple-choice questions in 3 hours, and the pass mark is 50%. It costs USD 240, the result is pass or fail only, and you get 3 attempts at DHA. It covers the same 10 areas as the Saudi and Qatar physiotherapy exams, from Kinesiology to Musculoskeletal Rehabilitation, and uses Qatar's reference books plus one more. DHA publishes no weights.

  1. Kinesiology, Biomechanics and Ergonomics
  2. Anatomy and Physiology
  3. Clinical Decision Making and Differential Diagnosis
  4. Physical Agents (Electrotherapy and Hydrotherapy)
  5. Therapeutic Exercises and Techniques
  6. Special Populations (OBGYN, Paediatrics, Geriatrics)

DHA publishes the content areas but no weights or question counts per area. Study all of them; don't skip an area because it looks small.

Question 1

During wrist flexion, the convex proximal carpal row moves on the concave radius. In which direction do the carpals glide?

  1. Dorsally
  2. Palmarly
  3. Radially
  4. Ulnarly
Show answer

A. Dorsally By the convex-concave rule, when a convex surface moves on a fixed concave surface, it rolls in the direction of the movement but glides in the opposite direction. At the radiocarpal joint, the convex proximal carpal row moves on the concave distal radius, so during wrist flexion the carpals roll palmarly and glide dorsally. To improve wrist flexion with joint mobilisation, the therapist therefore glides the carpals dorsally; a palmar glide is used to improve extension.

Source: University of Oklahoma HSC (D. Thompson) – Wrist

Question 2

When a person stands with the knee fully extended, the joint is locked. When they start to bend the knee from this position in weight-bearing, which motion unlocks it?

  1. Abduction of the tibia at the knee
  2. Medial rotation of the femur on the tibia
  3. Lateral rotation of the femur on the tibia
  4. Anterior glide of the femur on the tibia
Show answer

C. Lateral rotation of the femur on the tibia In the final degrees of extension with the foot planted, the femur rotates medially on the tibia, locking the knee so standing needs little muscle effort (the screw-home mechanism). Flexion begins with slight lateral rotation of the femur on the tibia, produced mainly by popliteus, which unlocks the knee. Medial femoral rotation is the locking motion, and anterior glide or abduction are not part of the mechanism.

Source: OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology (CC BY) 9.6 Anatomy of Selected Synovial Joints

Question 3

A footballer was kicked in the left upper abdomen. An hour later he reports left shoulder-tip pain with no shoulder injury, and he looks pale. What should the therapist suspect first?

  1. Strain of the supraspinatus tendon
  2. Irritation of the C4 nerve root
  3. Splenic injury with diaphragm irritation
  4. Sprain of the acromioclavicular joint
Show answer

C. Splenic injury with diaphragm irritation Left shoulder-tip pain after abdominal trauma is Kehr's sign: blood from a ruptured spleen irritates the underside of the diaphragm, and the pain is referred along the phrenic nerve (C3–C5) to the shoulder. Combined with pallor, it suggests internal bleeding and needs emergency referral. Shoulder or neck musculoskeletal problems would usually be linked to shoulder or neck movement, which does not fit this history.

Source: Physiopedia: Kehr's Sign

Question 4

A therapist is screening patients for extracorporeal shockwave therapy (ESWT). In which case should ESWT NOT be applied to the area?

  1. A malignant tumour in the field
  2. Chronic plantar heel pain (fasciopathy)
  3. Chronic lateral elbow tendinopathy
  4. Calcific tendinopathy of the shoulder
Show answer

A. A malignant tumour in the field ESWT uses acoustic pressure waves and is mainly used for chronic tendinopathies and plantar heel pain, including calcific tendinopathy of the shoulder and chronic lateral elbow pain. Treating over a malignant tumour is an absolute contraindication, as are active infection in the area and pregnancy with the fetus in the field. The other three options are common reasons to use ESWT, not reasons to withhold it.

Source: Physiopedia: Extracorporeal Shockwave Therapy (ESWT)

Question 5

A patient is given a TENS machine to use at home for chronic neck pain and asks how long to use it at first. Which advice is most appropriate?

  1. Use it for 24 hours without a break
  2. Use it for 2 minutes only, once a week
  3. Start with 30–60 min, then build up
  4. Use it only when the pain is at its worst
Show answer

C. Start with 30–60 min, then build up TENS can be used for as long as it helps, and some people wear it for much of the day. Patients are usually advised to begin with short periods of 30 to 60 minutes and increase as needed, while checking the skin under the electrodes. Intensity should give a strong but comfortable tingling, and it may need to be turned up as the person gets used to the sensation. Very brief or rare use is unlikely to help, and continuous use without checks risks skin problems.

Source: Cambridge University Hospitals: Advice on using a TENS machine

Question 6

The therapist positions a patient's hip precisely, then asks for a gentle, controlled contraction in a specific direction against the therapist's matching resistance before taking up the new range. Which technique is this?

  1. Passive static stretching
  2. Ballistic stretching
  3. Positional release
  4. Muscle energy technique
Show answer

D. Muscle energy technique Muscle energy technique uses the patient's own active contraction, from a precisely controlled position and in a specific direction, against a counterforce from the therapist, followed by relaxation and movement to a new barrier. It is used to lengthen muscles and improve joint motion. Passive static stretching needs no patient contraction, positional release places tissues in a shortened, comfortable position, and ballistic stretching uses bouncing movements.

Source: Wikipedia: Muscle energy technique

Question 7

A cardiac rehabilitation patient's programme alternates 1 minute of hard cycling with 2 minutes of easy pedalling, repeated several times. What type of training is this?

  1. Circuit resistance training
  2. High-intensity interval training
  3. Plyometric jump training
  4. Continuous moderate-intensity training
Show answer

B. High-intensity interval training High-intensity interval training alternates short bouts of hard effort with periods of easier recovery. It can improve aerobic fitness in a short time and, when supervised and screened, is used in cardiac rehabilitation. Continuous moderate training keeps a steady intensity for the whole session, circuit resistance training rotates through strength stations, and plyometric training uses rapid stretch-shortening jumps.

Source: Wikipedia: High-intensity interval training

Question 8

A patient with severe spasticity needs pressure relief, but reclining his hip angle triggers extensor spasms. Which wheelchair feature is most suitable?

  1. A lightweight folding frame
  2. A reclining back that opens the hip
  3. Tilt-in-space, keeping the hip angle
  4. Removable desk-length armrests
Show answer

C. Tilt-in-space, keeping the hip angle A tilt-in-space chair rotates the whole seat and back together on an arc, keeping the hip and knee angles the same while moving the patient from upright to recumbent. This redistributes pressure without stretching the hip into extension, which can trigger extensor spasms or cause sliding and shear. A reclining back opens the hip angle, and a lightweight frame or armrest style does not provide pressure relief.

Source: Lane Community College PTA course notes: Wheelchairs (PTA 101)

Question 9

A healthy woman at 20 weeks of pregnancy has brief, sharp pain in the lower abdomen and groin when she stands up quickly or rolls over in bed. She has no bleeding or fever. What is the most likely cause?

  1. Symphysis pubis dysfunction
  2. Appendicitis
  3. Placental abruption
  4. Round ligament pain
Show answer

D. Round ligament pain Round ligament pain is common in the second trimester as the uterus grows and stretches the round ligaments. It causes brief, sharp lower abdominal or groin pain with sudden movements such as standing, rolling or coughing, and eases with rest and slower position changes. Placental abruption causes bleeding and constant pain, symphysis pubis dysfunction causes pain at the front of the pelvis with single-leg loading, and appendicitis brings constant pain and fever. Red flags need urgent obstetric review.

Source: Wikipedia: Round ligament pain

Question 10

At rest, which nervous input keeps the heart rate lower than the intrinsic rate of the SA node?

  1. Somatic motor nerves to the heart
  2. Sympathetic cardiac accelerator nerves
  3. Parasympathetic input via the vagus nerve
  4. The phrenic nerve
Show answer

C. Parasympathetic input via the vagus nerve Left to itself, the SA node would fire at roughly 100 beats per minute. At rest, parasympathetic (vagal) input releases acetylcholine at the SA and AV nodes, slowing depolarisation and keeping the heart rate lower, typically 60–80 bpm. Sympathetic cardiac nerves increase rate and force, the heart has no somatic motor supply, and the phrenic nerve supplies the diaphragm.

Source: OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology (CC BY) 19.4 Cardiac Physiology

Question 11

The examiner flexes the patient's shoulder to 90° with the elbow bent, then forcibly rotates the arm internally, reproducing pain over the superolateral shoulder. Which test is this?

  1. Hawkins-Kennedy test
  2. Speed's test
  3. Drop arm test
  4. Apprehension test
Show answer

A. Hawkins-Kennedy test The Hawkins-Kennedy test places the shoulder in 90 degrees of flexion with the elbow flexed, then forcibly internally rotates the arm, driving the greater tubercle and supraspinatus tendon under the coracoacromial ligament. Pain suggests subacromial pain or impingement. It is sensitive but not very specific, so it is combined with other tests. The apprehension test checks anterior instability, Speed's test the biceps tendon, and the drop arm test a large rotator cuff tear.

Source: Lane Community College PTA course notes: Shoulder (PTA 104)

Question 12

On ISNCSCI testing, a patient with a lumbar spinal cord injury cannot dorsiflex his ankles. Which myotome do the ankle dorsiflexors represent?

  1. L2
  2. S1
  3. L3
  4. L4
Show answer

D. L4 In the International Standards, the ankle dorsiflexors are the key muscle for L4. The hip flexors represent L2, the knee extensors L3, the long toe extensors L5 and the ankle plantar flexors S1. Weak dorsiflexors cause foot drop, which patients may compensate for with hip hiking or a high-stepping gait to clear the toes.

Source: International Standards for Neurological Classification of SCI (ISNCSCI) worksheet – NINDS Common Data Elements

Question 13

A 28-year-old cyclist is brought in after a crash with a Glasgow Coma Scale score of 7. How is the severity of this brain injury classified?

  1. Mild
  2. Normal consciousness
  3. Severe
  4. Moderate
Show answer

C. Severe The Glasgow Coma Scale adds the best eye opening, verbal and motor responses to give a total from 3 to 15. A score of 8 or less indicates a severe brain injury, 9 to 12 a moderate injury and 13 to 15 a mild injury. A score of 7 therefore places this patient in the severe group, who usually needs airway protection and intensive care before rehabilitation can start. Normal consciousness corresponds to 15.

Source: BrainLine: What Is the Glasgow Coma Scale?

Question 14

After a head injury, a patient has vertical double vision that worsens when walking down stairs. Which cranial nerve and muscle are most likely affected?

  1. Trochlear nerve, superior oblique
  2. Abducens nerve, lateral rectus
  3. Optic nerve, ciliary muscle
  4. Facial nerve, orbicularis oculi
Show answer

A. Trochlear nerve, superior oblique The trochlear nerve (IV) supplies the superior oblique, which rotates the eye medially and helps move it down and in, as when looking down at stairs or reading. A trochlear palsy, a common result of head injury, causes vertical double vision that is worst looking down and inward, and patients often tilt their head away from the affected side. The abducens nerve supplies the lateral rectus (horizontal diplopia), the facial nerve closes the eyelids, and the optic nerve carries vision.

Source: OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology (CC BY) 16.3 The Cranial Nerve Exam

Question 15

A 45-year-old woman with rheumatoid arthritis attends during a flare. Her wrists and MCP joints are warm and swollen. Why should vigorous stretching of these joints be avoided now?

  1. Swelling makes the capsule too stiff to stretch
  2. Stretching provokes new osteophyte formation
  3. Stretching speeds up loss of bone density
  4. Swollen joints are prone to subluxation
Show answer

D. Swollen joints are prone to subluxation During an RA flare the synovium is inflamed and the capsule and ligaments are distended and weakened, so forceful stretching can sublux or damage the joint. Treatment then focuses on rest, splinting, gentle range of motion within pain limits and joint protection, and stretching resumes when swelling settles. Stretching does not cause bone loss or osteophytes, and a swollen capsule is lax and vulnerable rather than stiff.

Source: Lane Community College PTA course notes: Osteoarthritis and Rheumatoid Arthritis (PTA 104)
