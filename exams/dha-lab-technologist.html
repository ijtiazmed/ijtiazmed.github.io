DHA exam for lab technologists (LAB5631) guide

Last checked against official documents: 17 November 2026

The Dubai Health Authority (DHA) Medical Laboratory Technologist exam (code LAB5631) has 150 multiple-choice questions in 3 hours, and the pass mark is 50%. It costs USD 240, the result is pass or fail only, and you get 3 attempts at DHA. It covers the same 9 areas as the Saudi SLLE and the Qatar lab technologist exam. Lab Technician (LAB5601) is a separate DHA exam.

Question 1

A splenectomised patient develops fever and haemolysis 3 weeks after transfusion. The blood film shows small ring forms inside red cells, some arranged as tetrads, with no pigment. Which infection was most likely transmitted?

  1. Babesia
  2. Plasmodium falciparum
  3. Trypanosoma cruzi
  4. Leishmania donovani
Show answer

A. Babesia Babesia is an intraerythrocytic parasite spread by ticks and also by blood transfusion; severe disease occurs in asplenic patients. The film shows small rings without pigment, and tetrads (Maltese cross) are unique to Babesia. P. falciparum rings are similar, but tetrads are not seen and pigment and banana-shaped gametocytes may be. Trypanosoma cruzi appears as extracellular trypomastigotes. Leishmania amastigotes are inside macrophages, not red cells.

Source: MSD Manual Professional – Babesiosis

Question 2

A nurse wants to run a unit of red cells through the same IV line as a lactated Ringer's infusion. Why should this not be done?

  1. Glucose makes the red cells clump together
  2. Lactate haemolyses the stored red cells in the line
  3. Its potassium causes cardiac arrhythmia
  4. Calcium reverses citrate and clots the blood
Show answer

D. Calcium reverses citrate and clots the blood Stored blood is anticoagulated with citrate, which binds calcium. Calcium-containing fluids such as lactated Ringer's can overcome the citrate and cause clots to form in the bag or the line. Glucose-containing fluids such as 5% dextrose can cause red cell clumping and haemolysis, so 0.9% sodium chloride is the usual compatible fluid. Drugs should not be added to blood components either.

Source: WebPath (University of Utah) – Lab Medicine curriculum: Transfusion patient safety

Question 3

A patient with chronic diarrhoea and weight loss has a D-xylose absorption test with a low 5-hour urine xylose. What does this suggest?

  1. Pancreatic exocrine insufficiency
  2. Normal intestinal function
  3. Lactase deficiency only
  4. Small intestinal malabsorption
Show answer

D. Small intestinal malabsorption D-xylose is a sugar that is absorbed by the small intestine without needing pancreatic enzymes, then excreted in the urine. A low urine xylose after an oral dose suggests a problem with the small intestinal lining, such as coeliac disease. In pancreatic insufficiency, xylose absorption is normal while fat absorption is poor. Kidney impairment and incomplete urine collection can also give falsely low results.

Source: WebPath (University of Utah) – Lab Medicine curriculum: Hepatic, Pancreatic and GI tract

Question 4

An ICU patient on dopamine and high-dose glucocorticoids has a low TSH, low T3 and low-normal free T4, with no thyroid history. What is the most likely explanation?

  1. Primary hyperthyroidism
  2. Euthyroid sick syndrome
  3. Primary hypothyroidism
  4. Thyroid hormone resistance
Show answer

B. Euthyroid sick syndrome In severe non-thyroidal illness (euthyroid sick syndrome), T3 falls, reverse T3 rises, free T4 may be normal or low, and TSH can be normal, low or high. Dopamine and glucocorticoids directly suppress TSH, so a low TSH in very sick patients on these drugs does not mean hyperthyroidism. Primary hyperthyroidism would raise free T4 and T3, and primary hypothyroidism would raise TSH.

Source: MSD Manual Professional – Overview of Thyroid Function

Question 5

A 30-year-old woman with cyclic pelvic pain has a mildly raised CA-125. Imaging shows ovarian endometriomas and no mass suspicious for cancer. What best explains the result?

  1. The sample must be haemolysed
  2. CA-125 is specific for ovarian cancer
  3. Endometriosis can raise CA-125
  4. CA-125 is raised by fasting
Show answer

C. Endometriosis can raise CA-125 CA-125 can be mildly raised in benign conditions such as endometriosis, pelvic inflammatory disease, pregnancy, fibroids and peritoneal inflammation, so it is not specific for ovarian cancer. This is one reason it is not used for screening. Haemolysis and fasting are not recognised causes of raised CA-125.

Source: MSD Manual Professional – Ovarian, Fallopian Tube, and Peritoneal Cancer

Question 6

A patient has a raised ALP. Bone disease is also possible. Which additional enzyme, if raised, points to a hepatobiliary source?

  1. Creatine kinase
  2. 5'-nucleotidase
  3. Amylase
  4. Acid phosphatase
Show answer

B. 5'-nucleotidase Alkaline phosphatase comes from liver, bone, placenta and intestine. GGT or 5'-nucleotidase can be measured to confirm a hepatobiliary source: a raised 5'-nucleotidase almost always indicates hepatobiliary dysfunction, as in cholestasis. CK reflects muscle, amylase the pancreas and salivary glands, and acid phosphatase the prostate and some blood cells.

Source: MSD Manual Professional – Laboratory Tests of the Liver and Gallbladder

Question 7

Before surgery, a man has a markedly prolonged aPTT, a normal PT and no bleeding history. Mixing corrects the aPTT, and factor XII activity is 2%. What is the bleeding risk?

  1. Risk of delayed bleeding like FXIII
  2. Severe risk, like haemophilia A
  3. Risk only with aspirin use
  4. No increased bleeding risk
Show answer

D. No increased bleeding risk Factor XII (Hageman factor) is a contact factor needed for the aPTT test, so its deficiency markedly prolongs the aPTT, but most people do not bleed, even after major surgery, and no treatment is needed. Correction on mixing shows a factor deficiency rather than an inhibitor. Haemophilia and factor XIII deficiency do cause bleeding.

Source: National Bleeding Disorders Foundation – Factor XII Deficiency

Question 8

A patient is on an unfractionated heparin infusion monitored by aPTT. What is the usual therapeutic target?

  1. An INR of 2–3
  2. Within the normal reference range
  3. 5–6 × the control value
  4. 1.5–2.5 × the control value
Show answer

D. 1.5–2.5 × the control value The aPTT is prolonged by unfractionated heparin and is used to monitor it, with a common therapeutic target of 1.5 to 2.5 times the control value (each laboratory should set its own range, ideally matched to anti-Xa levels). A normal aPTT means too little heparin, 5–6 times control means a high bleeding risk, and the INR is used for warfarin.

Source: Testing.com (ADLM) – Partial Thromboplastin Time (PTT, aPTT)

Question 9

Which red cell finding is typical of thalassaemia trait, helping to separate it from iron deficiency?

  1. Macrocytes with a high MCV
  2. RBC count low for the Hb level
  3. RBC count high for the Hb level
  4. Very high RDW with pencil cells
Show answer

C. RBC count high for the Hb level In thalassaemia, the RBC count is elevated relative to the haemoglobin, and the cells are very microcytic. In iron deficiency the RBC count is usually low and the RDW is high, with pencil cells on the film. This difference is the basis of indices such as the Mentzer index (MCV/RBC). Macrocytes are not a feature of thalassaemia trait.

Source: MSD Manual Professional – Thalassemias

Question 10

A laboratory sets up a flow cytometric lymphocyte crossmatch. Under UK guidelines, which negative control should be used?

  1. The patient's own serum after heat treatment
  2. Pooled sera known not to react with lymphocytes
  3. Saline instead of serum
  4. A serum with only anti-A and anti-B
Show answer

B. Pooled sera known not to react with lymphocytes The flow cytometric crossmatch detects donor-specific HLA antibodies in a patient's serum. UK guidelines advise a negative control serum made from a pool of sera already shown not to react with lymphocytes by flow cytometry, at least one positive control, and testing each patient's serum in duplicate. Saline lacks the normal serum proteins that bind cells nonspecifically, and ABO antibodies do not test HLA reactivity.

Source: JPAC Red Book – 16.7 Leucocyte crossmatching in blood transfusion

Question 11

A lung biopsy from an HIV patient is stained to show Pneumocystis and fungal walls in black. Which stain is used?

  1. Perls Prussian blue reaction
  2. Grocott methenamine silver
  3. Masson trichrome stain
  4. Congo red stain
Show answer

B. Grocott methenamine silver Grocott (Gomori) methenamine silver stains fungal cell walls and Pneumocystis cysts black against a green background, and also stains basement membranes, so it is the standard stain for fungi and Pneumocystis in tissue. It is more sensitive than H&E, on which many fungi are hard to see. Congo red shows amyloid. Masson trichrome shows collagen. Perls Prussian blue shows ferric iron.

Source: Leica Biosystems – An Introduction to Routine and Special Staining

Question 12

Blood cultures from a patient with subacute endocarditis after dental work grow Gram-positive cocci in chains. On blood agar the colonies have a green zone around them. What type of haemolysis is this?

  1. Alpha haemolysis
  2. Beta haemolysis
  3. Gamma haemolysis
  4. Double-zone haemolysis
Show answer

A. Alpha haemolysis A green zone around colonies is alpha haemolysis, caused by partial breakdown of haemoglobin to biliverdin-like products. Viridans streptococci (and S. pneumoniae) are alpha-haemolytic, and viridans streptococci are a classic cause of subacute endocarditis after dental procedures. Beta haemolysis is complete clearing (for example, S. pyogenes). Gamma means no haemolysis. Double-zone haemolysis is typical of Clostridium perfringens.

Source: MSD Manual Professional – Streptococcal Infections

Question 13

A neutropenic patient with suspected invasive aspergillosis has repeatedly negative blood cultures. How should this be interpreted?

  1. Blood cultures confirm aspergillosis if negative
  2. Aspergillosis is excluded
  3. The lab must be using the wrong media
  4. Expected; blood cultures are almost always negative
Show answer

D. Expected; blood cultures are almost always negative Aspergillus rarely grows in blood cultures, even in disseminated disease or endocarditis, so negative blood cultures do not exclude invasive aspergillosis. Diagnosis relies on imaging, galactomannan (especially on BAL fluid), PCR and tissue histology and culture. A negative blood culture does not confirm or exclude the disease, and the problem is not one of culture media.

Source: MSD Manual Professional – Aspergillosis

Question 14

Guests at a wedding develop vomiting and cramps 3 hours after eating cream-filled pastries left at room temperature. Why do symptoms start so quickly?

  1. Invasion of the gut wall
  2. Toxin made only after colonisation
  3. Spores germinating in the colon
  4. A preformed enterotoxin in the food
Show answer

D. A preformed enterotoxin in the food Staphylococcal food poisoning is caused by a preformed enterotoxin produced by S. aureus that multiplies in food left at room temperature. Because the toxin is already present, symptoms begin within 30 minutes to 8 hours, usually with nausea, vomiting and cramps, and resolve within about a day. Invasive organisms (for example, Shigella) and organisms that must colonise first have longer incubation periods.

Source: MSD Manual Professional – Staphylococcal Food Poisoning

Question 15

A laboratory reference interval is set to include the central 95% of results from healthy people. If 20 unrelated tests are run on a healthy person, what is expected?

  1. All results will be inside the reference interval
  2. About 1 result may fall outside the interval by chance
  3. About half of the results will be outside the interval
  4. Any result outside the interval proves disease
Show answer

B. About 1 result may fall outside the interval by chance Because reference intervals usually cover the central 95% of a healthy population, about 5% of healthy results fall outside them by chance. Across 20 independent tests, about 1 abnormal result is expected in a healthy person. All results being inside is not guaranteed. Half being outside would be far too many. A result just outside the interval is not proof of disease and must be interpreted clinically.

Source: Testing.com (ADLM) – Laboratory Test Reference Ranges

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are in the DHA lab technologist exam, and how long is it?

150 multiple-choice questions. Time: 3 hours, including registration and the introduction.

What is the DHA lab technologist exam pass mark?

The pass mark is 50%, but DHA reports the result only as pass or fail, without a score.

What topics does the DHA lab technologist exam cover?

Nine areas: Blood Bank; Urinalysis and Body Fluids; Clinical Chemistry; Hematology; Immunology and Serology; Histo- and Cyto-Techniques; Microbiology; Laboratory Operations; and Patient Safety and Professionalism.

Is the DHA Lab Technician exam the same as Lab Technologist?

No. Lab Technician (LAB5601) is a separate DHA exam with its own list of 12 areas, including bacteriology, virology, parasitology, mycology and hormones.
