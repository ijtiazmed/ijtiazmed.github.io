Qatar pharmacist exam (NPQE) guide

Last checked against official documents: 17 November 2026

Qatar's National Pharmacist Qualifying Exam (NPQE) is run by the Department of Healthcare Professions (DHP, formerly QCHP) through Prometric, with new content since 1 May 2025. It has 150 multiple-choice questions in 3 hours, and the pass mark is 60%. Clinical Sciences has the most questions (67), followed by Pharmaceutical Sciences (38), Social, Behavioral and Administrative Sciences (38) and Basic Biomedical Sciences (7). Its primary references include four Qatar Ministry of Public Health documents.

Try 15 practice questions

Question 1

A newborn develops poor feeding, lethargy and urine with a sweet, caramel-like odour. Which amino acids cannot be broken down normally in this disorder?

  1. Phenylalanine and tyrosine only
  2. Methionine and cysteine only
  3. Glycine, serine and alanine
  4. Leucine, isoleucine and valine
Show answer

D. Leucine, isoleucine and valine Maple syrup urine disease is an autosomal recessive defect of the branched-chain alpha-ketoacid dehydrogenase complex, preventing the normal breakdown of the branched-chain amino acids leucine, isoleucine and valine. It is named for the sweet odour of affected infants' urine. Untreated, it causes poor feeding, vomiting, encephalopathy, seizures, coma and death. Treatment is a lifelong diet restricted in branched-chain amino acids, with urgent management during illness; some forms respond to thiamine.

Source: MedlinePlus Genetics (NIH) – Maple syrup urine disease

Question 2

During a 20-minute IV infusion of vancomycin 1 g, a patient develops itching and redness of the face, neck and upper chest, with no wheeze or hypotension. What is the most appropriate action?

  1. Stop it and record a vancomycin allergy
  2. Give it more slowly, over at least 60 minutes
  3. Switch to oral vancomycin for the infection
  4. Give intramuscular epinephrine at once
Show answer

B. Give it more slowly, over at least 60 minutes This is the vancomycin infusion reaction (formerly 'red man syndrome'), caused by histamine release when the drug is infused too fast. It is not a true allergy. Vancomycin should be given as a diluted infusion over 60 minutes or more, and an antihistamine may help. Oral vancomycin is not absorbed and would not treat a systemic infection. Epinephrine is for anaphylaxis, which is not present here.

Source: DailyMed – Vancomycin Hydrochloride for Injection (label)

Question 3

During a C. difficile outbreak on a ward, how should staff clean their hands after caring for an affected patient?

  1. No hand hygiene is needed if gloves were worn
  2. Use alcohol-based rub only
  3. Use an alcohol rub with 95% ethanol
  4. Wash with soap and water
Show answer

D. Wash with soap and water Alcohol does not kill C. difficile spores, so alcohol-based rubs are less effective against them. During outbreaks, CDC encourages hand washing with soap and water after caring for patients with known or suspected infection, because friction and rinsing physically remove spores. Gloves and gowns are still used, but hand hygiene is needed after removing gloves. A higher alcohol concentration does not make spores susceptible.

Source: CDC – Clinical Safety: Hand Hygiene for Healthcare Workers

Question 4

A patient starting orlistat for weight loss asks about vitamin supplements. What is the correct advice?

  1. Avoid all multivitamins, as they reduce orlistat's effect
  2. Take a vitamin C tablet together with every orlistat dose
  3. Take a daily A, D, E, K multivitamin 2 h apart from it
  4. Take vitamin B12 injections monthly during treatment
Show answer

C. Take a daily A, D, E, K multivitamin 2 h apart from it Orlistat inhibits gastric and pancreatic lipases, so about 30% of dietary fat is not absorbed. Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) and beta-carotene are absorbed with fat, so their absorption also falls. Patients should take a daily multivitamin containing them, 2 hours before or after orlistat or at bedtime. Fatty, oily stools and urgency are common if meals are high in fat.

Source: MedlinePlus (NIH) – Orlistat

Question 5

A patient taking bismuth subsalicylate (Pepto-Bismol) for diarrhoea is alarmed that his tongue and stools have turned black. What is the explanation?

  1. Upper gastrointestinal bleeding in every case
  2. A temporary, harmless effect of the bismuth
  3. An allergic reaction to the tablet coating
  4. Liver failure from the salicylate content
Show answer

B. A temporary, harmless effect of the bismuth Bismuth reacts with sulfur in the mouth and gut to form black bismuth sulfide, causing a temporary, harmless darkening of the tongue and stool. Patients should still seek advice if they have other signs of bleeding. Because it contains a salicylate, it should not be used by children or teenagers recovering from chickenpox or flu-like illness (Reye's syndrome risk), and patients on anticoagulants or with aspirin allergy should ask first.

Source: DailyMed – Pepto-Bismol Chews (bismuth subsalicylate) (OTC Drug Facts)

Question 6

An older man with overactive bladder cannot tolerate anticholinergics, and mirabegron is started. Which parameter should be monitored?

  1. Blood glucose
  2. Serum potassium
  3. Thyroid function
  4. Blood pressure
Show answer

D. Blood pressure Mirabegron is a beta-3 adrenergic agonist that relaxes the detrusor muscle during bladder filling. It avoids anticholinergic effects such as dry mouth, constipation and confusion, which suits older adults. However, it can increase blood pressure, so blood pressure is checked before and during treatment, and it is avoided in severe uncontrolled hypertension. It can also cause urinary retention, especially with bladder outlet obstruction or with antimuscarinics.

Source: MedlinePlus (NIH) – Mirabegron

Question 7

In a case-control study of a rare liver injury, 40 of 100 cases and 20 of 100 controls had used a certain herbal product. What is the odds ratio for herbal use?

  1. 2.0
  2. 2.67
  3. 3.2
  4. 0.38
Show answer

B. 2.67 Odds ratio = (a × d) ÷ (b × c), where a = exposed cases (40), b = exposed controls (20), c = unexposed cases (60) and d = unexposed controls (80). OR = (40 × 80) ÷ (20 × 60) = 3,200 ÷ 1,200 = 2.67. 2.0 is the ratio of the exposure proportions (40% ÷ 20%), not the odds. 0.38 is the inverted ratio. Because the outcome is rare, the OR approximates the risk ratio.

Source: CDC – Principles of Epidemiology in Public Health Practice, 3rd ed. (Lesson 3)

Question 8

A hospital pharmacist in Qatar leading an antimicrobial stewardship project wants to link it to the national Infection Prevention and Control / Antimicrobial Resistance programme. Which MOPH department runs that programme?

  1. Pharmacy and Drug Control Department
  2. Healthcare Quality Department
  3. Health Research Regulations Department
  4. Health Emergency Department
Show answer

B. Healthcare Quality Department The Healthcare Quality Department aims to ensure effective, safe and person-centred care for everyone in Qatar. It runs national programmes on Infection Prevention and Control and Antimicrobial Resistance, the National Clinical Guidelines, and Patient Safety and Risk Management. Pharmacy and Drug Control regulates medicines and pharmacies, Health Research Regulations oversees research, and Health Emergency handles emergency preparedness. An antimicrobial stewardship project therefore links to the Healthcare Quality programme (MOPH, Healthcare Quality Department page).

Source: MOPH Qatar – Healthcare Quality Department

Question 9

A clinical pharmacist in Qatar plans a study involving patients and needs it reviewed by a registered research review committee. Which MOPH department registers these committees and monitors health research?

  1. Pharmacy and Drug Control Department
  2. Department of Healthcare Professions
  3. Healthcare Quality Department
  4. Health Research Regulations Department
Show answer

D. Health Research Regulations Department The Health Research Regulations Department sets the legislation and policies for health research in Qatar. Its objectives include registering research review committees and issuing assurance certificates for human, animal and basic research, and inspecting research to ensure high scientific and ethical standards and the privacy of personal, health and genetic information. Healthcare professions licensing, quality programmes and medicine regulation belong to other departments, so research oversight goes through Health Research Regulations (MOPH, Health Research Regulatory Department page).

Source: MOPH Qatar – Health Research Regulatory Department

Question 10

A patient is to start an HIV regimen containing abacavir. Which test must be done first?

  1. CYP2D6 genotyping
  2. HLA-B*1502 screening
  3. G6PD enzyme activity
  4. HLA-B*5701 screening
Show answer

D. HLA-B*5701 screening Abacavir can cause a serious, sometimes fatal, multi-organ hypersensitivity reaction strongly associated with the HLA-B*5701 allele. All patients should be screened before starting abacavir, and it is contraindicated in those who are positive. After a suspected hypersensitivity reaction, abacavir or any abacavir-containing product must never be restarted. HLA-B*1502 screening applies to carbamazepine in at-risk Asian populations.

Source: DailyMed – Ziagen (abacavir) (label, boxed warning)

Question 11

A woman who is 30 weeks pregnant has confirmed influenza. Which antiviral does CDC prefer for treatment during pregnancy?

  1. Oral baloxavir
  2. Inhaled zanamivir
  3. IV peramivir
  4. Oral oseltamivir
Show answer

D. Oral oseltamivir Pregnant women are at higher risk of severe influenza and should be treated promptly. CDC states that oral oseltamivir is the preferred treatment during pregnancy, because it has the most safety data and is effective. Treatment should not be delayed for test results, and benefit is greatest when started early, though it can still help later in severe illness. Baloxavir is not recommended in pregnancy because of limited data.

Source: CDC – Influenza Antiviral Medications: Summary for Clinicians

Question 12

A man with diarrhoea for one day asks for loperamide. He mentions blood in his stool. What is the best response?

  1. Loperamide is fine at double the usual dose
  2. Do not use loperamide; refer him to a doctor
  3. Use loperamide and add an antacid
  4. Use loperamide for 2 weeks and then review
Show answer

B. Do not use loperamide; refer him to a doctor The OTC loperamide label states it should not be used if there is bloody or black stool, and a doctor should be consulted first if there is fever or mucus in the stool. Bloody diarrhoea suggests invasive infection (e.g. Shigella, Campylobacter, E. coli O157) or inflammatory bowel disease, and slowing the gut can worsen it or increase the risk of complications. Use should stop and advice be sought if diarrhoea lasts more than 2 days.

Source: DailyMed – Imodium A-D (loperamide) softgels (OTC Drug Facts)

Question 13

A patient with intermittent claudication is prescribed cilostazol. Which condition in his history makes it contraindicated?

  1. Osteoarthritis
  2. Heart failure
  3. Hypothyroidism
  4. Type 2 diabetes
Show answer

B. Heart failure Cilostazol is a phosphodiesterase-3 inhibitor. Other PDE3 inhibitors (such as milrinone) increased mortality in patients with heart failure, so cilostazol is contraindicated in heart failure of any severity. It is taken twice daily at least 30 minutes before or 2 hours after breakfast and dinner, and walking distance may take up to 12 weeks to improve fully. CYP3A4 and CYP2C19 inhibitors require dose reduction.

Source: MedlinePlus (NIH) – Cilostazol

Question 14

A 2-year-old has a very low weight for his height after recent severe diarrhoea. Which form of undernutrition does this describe?

  1. Micronutrient excess
  2. Stunting
  3. Obesity
  4. Wasting
Show answer

D. Wasting WHO describes low weight-for-height as wasting; it usually reflects recent, severe weight loss from too little food and/or infectious disease such as diarrhoea, and severe wasting carries a high risk of death but is treatable, for example with ready-to-use therapeutic food. Low height-for-age is stunting, the result of chronic or recurrent undernutrition. Low weight-for-age is underweight. Malnutrition also includes micronutrient-related problems and overweight.

Source: WHO – Malnutrition fact sheet

Question 15

A 25-year-old has acute hepatitis A after a trip abroad. He asks whether it will become a long-term liver disease. What is correct?

  1. Most adults develop chronic hepatitis within a year
  2. Lifelong antiviral therapy stops it becoming chronic
  3. Hepatitis A does not cause chronic liver disease
  4. It always leads to cirrhosis unless treated
Show answer

C. Hepatitis A does not cause chronic liver disease Hepatitis A is spread mainly by the faecal-oral route through contaminated food and water. Unlike hepatitis B and C, it does not cause chronic liver disease, and most people recover fully with lifelong immunity, although adults can be ill for weeks and acute liver failure occasionally occurs. There is no specific antiviral treatment; care is supportive, avoiding unnecessary medicines that can harm the liver. Vaccination prevents infection, and good hand hygiene limits spread to household contacts.

Source: WHO – Hepatitis A fact sheet

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are in the Qatar pharmacist exam (NPQE), and how long is it?

150 multiple-choice questions. Time: 3 hours, per DHP's exam page and Prometric (the older blueprint PDFs say 3.5 hours, so check your Prometric booking).

What is the Qatar pharmacist exam (NPQE) pass mark?

The pass mark is 60% of the questions.

What topics does the Qatar pharmacist exam (NPQE) cover?

Clinical Sciences (67 questions), Pharmaceutical Sciences (38), Social, Behavioral and Administrative Sciences (38) and Basic Biomedical Sciences (7). Compared with the Saudi SPLE, it is much more clinical and has less basic science.

Does the Qatar pharmacist exam test Qatari pharmacy law?

DHP's official reference list doesn't name any Qatari law. It lists four Ministry of Public Health documents: the Qatar National Health Strategy 2024–2030, Qatar National Health Services, the Qatar Dietary Guidelines, and the Ministry's departments.
