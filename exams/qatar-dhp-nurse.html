Qatar nursing exam (DHP Registered General Nurse) guide

Last checked against official documents: 17 November 2026

Qatar's Registered General Nurse qualifying exam is run by the Department of Healthcare Professions (DHP, formerly QCHP) through Prometric. It has 150 multiple-choice questions in 3 hours, and the pass mark is 50%. You can take it an unlimited number of times. Adult Nursing has the most questions (61), followed by Maternal-Child Nursing (44), Nursing Fundamentals (30) and Unit Management and Leadership (15).

Try 15 practice questions

Question 1

A nurse is teaching a patient with diabetes about foot care. Which footwear advice is correct?

  1. Wear tight shoes for support
  2. Walk barefoot at home to toughen the skin
  3. Go barefoot on the beach only
  4. Never go barefoot
Show answer

D. Never go barefoot CDC advises people with diabetes never to go barefoot, and to wear shoes, socks or slippers even indoors. Reduced feeling in the feet means injuries from stepping on objects may go unnoticed. Walking barefoot anywhere, including at home or on the beach, raises this risk, and tight shoes can cause blisters and pressure sores.

Source: CDC – Diabetes and your feet

Question 2

A client is receiving continuous feeding through a nasogastric tube. How should the nurse position the client?

  1. Head lowered slightly below the feet
  2. Lying prone with the head turned aside
  3. Head of bed raised at least 30 degrees
  4. Flat on the back with one thin pillow
Show answer

C. Head of bed raised at least 30 degrees Raising the head of the bed to at least 30 degrees (often 30–45 degrees) during and after enteral feeding uses gravity to keep feed in the stomach and reduces reflux and aspiration pneumonia. Lying flat, head-down or prone positions all increase the risk of regurgitation and aspiration. If the client must lie flat for a procedure, the feed is paused first.

Source: Open RN – Nursing Advanced Skills, 5.3 Applying the Nursing Process (NG tubes)

Question 3

A client reports pain in the right lower abdomen. When should the nurse palpate this area?

  1. First, before the other quadrants
  2. Only after giving analgesia
  3. Last, after the other quadrants
  4. Never; palpation is not allowed
Show answer

C. Last, after the other quadrants Painful areas are palpated last so the client does not tense the abdominal muscles (guarding) from the start, which would make the rest of the examination less accurate and more uncomfortable. Palpating it first causes guarding. Waiting for analgesia is not required and could mask findings. Gentle palpation is part of the assessment unless a specific condition contraindicates it.

Source: Open RN – Nursing Skills 2e (2023), 12.3 Gastrointestinal and Genitourinary Assessment

Question 4

A client feels a migraine starting. Which measure may help in the early stage?

  1. Skipping the next meal
  2. Strenuous exercise for 30 minutes
  3. Bright lights and loud music
  4. A small amount of caffeine
Show answer

D. A small amount of caffeine Small amounts of caffeine may help in the early stages of a migraine, along with resting in a dark, quiet room, applying a cool cloth to the forehead and taking prescribed medication early. Bright light and noise worsen migraine. Strenuous exercise can intensify the headache. Skipping meals is a common migraine trigger.

Source: Open RN – Health Alterations (2024), 9.12 Other Nervous System Disorders

Question 5

Two weeks after starting montelukast, a teenager's parents notice he is withdrawn and talking about not wanting to live. What should the nurse do?

  1. Report this urgently to the prescriber
  2. Reassure them that teenagers are moody
  3. Advise doubling the montelukast dose
  4. Stop the rescue inhaler as well
Show answer

A. Report this urgently to the prescriber Montelukast can cause serious neuropsychiatric effects, including mood changes and suicidal thoughts. New withdrawal and talk of not wanting to live must be reported urgently so the prescriber can review the drug and the young person's safety can be assessed. Dismissing it as normal teenage mood is unsafe. Increasing montelukast or stopping the rescue inhaler would be harmful.

Source: Open RN – Nursing Pharmacology 2e, 5.13 Leukotriene Receptor Antagonists

Question 6

A 60-year-old long-term smoker reports which group of symptoms that should prompt assessment for lung cancer?

  1. Joint stiffness for an hour each morning
  2. Persistent cough with blood, weight loss
  3. Sneezing, itchy eyes and a runny nose daily
  4. Heartburn and bloating after large meals
Show answer

B. Persistent cough with blood, weight loss Warning signs of lung cancer include a persistent cough, often with haemoptysis (coughing up blood), breathlessness, unexplained weight loss, fatigue and loss of appetite. In a long-term smoker these need prompt assessment. Sneezing and itchy eyes suggest allergy, heartburn suggests reflux and morning joint stiffness suggests arthritis.

Source: Open RN – Health Alterations (2024), 6.8 Lung Cancer

Question 7

A hospitalised client is diagnosed with norovirus gastroenteritis and is vomiting frequently. Which precautions should the nurse use in addition to standard precautions?

  1. Contact and droplet precautions
  2. Standard precautions only
  3. Airborne precautions only
  4. Protective (reverse) isolation
Show answer

A. Contact and droplet precautions Norovirus is highly contagious and spreads by the faecal-oral route and through contaminated surfaces, and vomiting can spread infectious droplets. Contact and droplet precautions are therefore used, with thorough handwashing with soap and water and environmental cleaning. Airborne precautions are for diseases like TB and measles. Standard precautions alone are not enough, and reverse isolation protects immunocompromised clients.

Source: Open RN – Health Alterations (2024), 11.7 Gastroenteritis

Question 8

A client prescribed amoxicillin every 8 hours for 7 days asks how to take it. What should the nurse teach?

  1. Stop once the fever has gone
  2. Take it only when symptoms are bad
  3. Space doses evenly and finish the course
  4. Take all daily doses together at night
Show answer

C. Space doses evenly and finish the course Antibiotics work best when blood levels are kept steady, so doses are spaced evenly around the clock and the whole course is completed, even if the client feels better. Taking it only when symptoms are bad, stopping when the fever settles or taking all doses at once leads to treatment failure and encourages antibiotic resistance.

Source: Open RN – Nursing Pharmacology 2e, 3.5 Penicillin

Question 9

Which ECG finding is characteristic of atrial flutter?

  1. Sawtooth atrial waves
  2. No visible waves at all
  3. Prolonged PR interval only
  4. Wide, bizarre QRS complexes
Show answer

A. Sawtooth atrial waves Atrial flutter shows rapid atrial activity as regular sawtooth flutter waves, and the PR interval cannot be measured. A flat line suggests asystole. Wide, bizarre QRS complexes suggest ventricular rhythms such as ventricular tachycardia or premature ventricular contractions. A prolonged PR interval alone is first-degree AV block.

Source: Open RN – Nursing Advanced Skills, 7.4 ECG Patterns and Dysrhythmias

Question 10

Which group lists the leading causes of newborn deaths worldwide?

  1. Road injuries and drowning
  2. Measles and polio
  3. Preterm birth, birth complications and infections
  4. Obesity, diabetes and high blood pressure in babies
Show answer

C. Preterm birth, birth complications and infections WHO names premature birth, birth complications such as asphyxia or trauma, neonatal infections and congenital anomalies as the leading causes of newborn deaths. Obesity and diabetes are adult conditions, injuries and drowning affect older children, and measles and polio are not the main causes of death in the first 28 days.

Source: WHO – Newborn mortality fact sheet (2024)

Question 11

Six hours after a vaginal birth, the nurse finds the fundus firm, 2 cm above the umbilicus and deviated to the right. What should the nurse do first?

  1. Help her empty her bladder
  2. Give an extra oxytocin dose
  3. Record it as a normal finding
  4. Massage the fundus firmly
Show answer

A. Help her empty her bladder A fundus that is high and pushed to one side usually means a full bladder is displacing the uterus. An overdistended bladder stops the uterus contracting well and increases the risk of postpartum haemorrhage, so the first step is to help the mother void, then reassess. The fundus is already firm, so massage and extra oxytocin are not the first steps. It is not a normal finding to ignore.

Source: Open RN – Nursing: Health Promotion, 11.2 Postpartum Physiological Adaptations

Question 12

A child in Qatar had the first MMR dose at 12 months. Under Qatar's national immunisation schedule, when is the second dose of MMR given?

  1. 18 months
  2. 15 months
  3. 2 years
  4. 4–6 years
Show answer

A. 18 months Qatar's schedule gives the second MMR dose at 18 months, six months after the first dose at 12 months, together with the second varicella dose. Qatar does not wait until 4–6 years, which is when some other countries give the second dose. 15 months and 2 years are not routine MMR visits in this schedule.

Source: Qatar Ministry of Public Health – National immunization schedule (schedule image on the MOPH Immunization page; file dated 8 Jul 2026)

Question 13

Under Qatar's current national immunisation schedule, when are the two doses of chickenpox (varicella) vaccine given?

  1. 9 months and 12 months
  2. 18 months and 4–6 years
  3. 12 months and 2 years
  4. 12 months and 18 months
Show answer

D. 12 months and 18 months Qatar's schedule gives varicella at 12 months (Varicella 1) and 18 months (Varicella 2), at the same visits as the two MMR doses. Older Qatar schedules gave the second dose at school age, so check the current schedule. 2 years is not a routine visit, starting at 18 months follows another country's schedule, and varicella, a live vaccine, is not given routinely at 9 months.

Source: Qatar Ministry of Public Health – National immunization schedule (schedule image on the MOPH Immunization page; file dated 8 Jul 2026)

Question 14

According to the clinical reasoning cycle, what does the nurse do after first considering the client's situation?

  1. Collect cues and information
  2. Reflect on the shift
  3. Evaluate the outcomes of care
  4. Take action immediately
Show answer

A. Collect cues and information The clinical reasoning cycle begins when the nurse considers the client situation and then collects cues and information. The nurse then processes the information, identifies problems, sets goals, takes action and evaluates outcomes, then reflects. Acting or evaluating before gathering cues skips essential steps.

Source: Open RN – Nursing Management and Professional Concepts 2e, 2.4 Critical Thinking and Clinical Reasoning

Question 15

A hospital receives higher payments when it meets quality targets and has good client outcomes. What is this payment model called?

  1. Pay for performance (value-based)
  2. Out-of-pocket payment by the client
  3. Fixed annual global budget payment
  4. Fee-for-service (per item) payment
Show answer

A. Pay for performance (value-based) Pay for performance, also called value-based payment, links financial rewards to the performance of health care agencies and providers on quality, process and outcome measures. Fee-for-service pays for each service provided regardless of outcome. A global budget is a fixed amount, and out-of-pocket payment is paid by the client.

Source: Open RN – Nursing Management and Professional Concepts 2e, 8.4 Health Care Reimbursement Models

Frequently asked questions

How many questions are in the Qatar DHP nurse exam, and how long is it?

150 multiple-choice questions. Time: 3 hours, per DHP's exam page and Prometric (the older blueprint PDFs say 3.5 hours, so check your Prometric booking).

What is the Qatar DHP nurse exam pass mark?

The pass mark is 50% of the questions.

What topics does the Qatar DHP nurse exam cover?

Adult Nursing (61 questions), Maternal-Child Nursing (44), Nursing Fundamentals (30) and Unit Management and Leadership (15). These are the same four domains, with almost the same weights, as the Saudi SNLE.

Is the Qatar nursing exam similar to the Saudi SNLE?

Yes. It uses the same four domains with almost the same weights. The main differences are the format (150 questions in one 3-hour sitting instead of 200 in two parts) and the 50% pass mark.
