Question 1

Benzodiazepines and barbiturates enhance the action of which major inhibitory neurotransmitter of the central nervous system?

  1. Glutamate (via NMDA receptors)
  2. Aspartate (an amino acid transmitter)
  3. Gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA)
  4. Acetylcholine (via nicotinic receptors)
أظهر الإجابة

C. Gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) GABA and glycine are inhibitory amino acid neurotransmitters: their receptors hyperpolarise the postsynaptic neuron and make it less likely to fire. Benzodiazepines and barbiturates act at the GABA-A receptor and increase its inhibitory chloride current, which explains their sedative and anticonvulsant effects. Glutamate and aspartate are excitatory amino acids, and acetylcholine is mainly excitatory at nicotinic and many muscarinic sites.

Source: OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e – 12.5 Communication Between Neurons

Question 2

A patient with type 2 diabetes on gliclazide wants to start Asian ginseng for energy. What should the pharmacist warn about?

  1. Ginseng greatly raises blood glucose
  2. Ginseng turns the urine dark blue
  3. Ginseng causes severe hyperkalaemia
  4. Ginseng may lower blood glucose further
أظهر الإجابة

D. Ginseng may lower blood glucose further Some evidence suggests that Asian ginseng (Panax ginseng) might lower blood sugar, so combining it with sulfonylureas or insulin could increase the risk of hypoglycaemia. Patients with diabetes should discuss it with a healthcare provider and monitor glucose more closely. Insomnia is the most common side effect. Ginseng may also interact with other medicines, including warfarin and MAO inhibitors, and long-term safety data are limited.

Source: NCCIH (NIH) – Asian ginseng

Question 3

A patient taking repaglinide before each meal plans to skip lunch. What should he do?

  1. Take a double dose at dinner
  2. Stop repaglinide for the day
  3. Take the dose anyway
  4. Skip the lunchtime dose
أظهر الإجابة

D. Skip the lunchtime dose Repaglinide is a short-acting insulin secretagogue taken up to 30 minutes before meals, so it is matched to each meal. If a meal is skipped, the dose should also be skipped to avoid hypoglycaemia; if an extra meal is added, an extra dose may be taken. Doubling later doses is unsafe. Gemfibrozil and clopidogrel inhibit CYP2C8 and greatly raise repaglinide levels.

Source: MedlinePlus (NIH) – Repaglinide

Question 4

A patient prescribed eszopiclone for insomnia usually eats a heavy, fatty dinner just before bed. What advice is correct?

  1. Take it in the morning instead of at night
  2. Take it with the meal to prevent any nausea
  3. Take it with warm milk to increase its effect
  4. Do not take it with or just after a heavy meal
أظهر الإجابة

D. Do not take it with or just after a heavy meal A heavy, high-fat meal slows eszopiclone absorption and delays its onset, so it should not be taken with or shortly after such a meal. It is taken immediately before bedtime, only when the patient can stay in bed for 7–8 hours. Common effects include an unpleasant (metallic) taste and next-morning impairment. Complex sleep behaviours such as sleep-driving are a boxed warning; if they occur, the drug must be stopped. Alcohol must be avoided.

Source: MedlinePlus (NIH) – Eszopiclone

Question 5

A patient with CKD taking spironolactone starts drinking noni juice daily. Which laboratory problem is most likely?

  1. Hypoglycaemia
  2. Hyperkalaemia
  3. Hypercalcaemia
  4. Hyponatraemia
أظهر الإجابة

B. Hyperkalaemia Noni (Morinda citrifolia) contains a substantial amount of potassium. In people with kidney disease, or those taking potassium-sparing diuretics such as spironolactone, ACE inhibitors or ARBs, extra potassium can cause hyperkalaemia, which can lead to dangerous arrhythmias. Such patients should consult a healthcare provider before using noni. Several cases of liver toxicity have also been reported in people who drank noni juice or tea for several weeks.

Source: NCCIH (NIH) – Noni

Question 6

A 10-year-old with cerebral palsy is prescribed glycopyrrolate oral solution. What is it most likely being used for?

  1. Increasing appetite and weight
  2. Treating spasticity in the legs
  3. Preventing seizures at night
  4. Reducing saliva and drooling
أظهر الإجابة

D. Reducing saliva and drooling Glycopyrrolate is a quaternary antimuscarinic that does not readily cross the blood–brain barrier. The oral solution is used to reduce chronic severe drooling in children aged 3–16 with neurological conditions such as cerebral palsy, and tablets are used for peptic ulcer in adults. It is taken on an empty stomach. Because it reduces sweating, children should avoid overheating, and constipation and urinary retention should be watched for.

Source: MedlinePlus (NIH) – Glycopyrrolate

Question 7

Spread of influenza through large respiratory droplets when an infected person coughs on someone within about a metre is classified as which type of transmission?

  1. Vehicle-borne transmission
  2. Direct transmission
  3. Vector-borne transmission
  4. Indirect airborne transmission
أظهر الإجابة

B. Direct transmission Direct transmission is the immediate transfer of an agent from a reservoir to a host by direct contact or droplet spread. Large droplets travel only a short distance before falling. Indirect transmission includes airborne spread on small particles that stay suspended in the air (e.g. measles, tuberculosis), vehicle-borne spread on objects such as food or water, and vector-borne spread by insects. These distinctions guide infection control precautions.

Source: CDC – Principles of Epidemiology in Public Health Practice, 3rd ed. (Glossary)

Question 8

A pharmacist receives a prescription for methotrexate 10 mg to be taken daily for rheumatoid arthritis, which he suspects should be weekly. Under UAE federal law, what must he do?

  1. Contact the treating physician to clarify and correct it
  2. Dispense it weekly and explain the change to the patient
  3. Dispense it as written, since the physician is responsible
  4. Return the prescription to the patient without comment
أظهر الإجابة

A. Contact the treating physician to clarify and correct it Article 80 obliges the pharmacist to contact the treating physician if he suspects or finds a fundamental error in a prescription, to clarify it and ask for correction if needed. Changing the frequency himself would breach Article 78, which bars altering a prescription without the prescriber's written opinion. Dispensing a suspected dangerous error as written, or handing it back without explanation, fails the duty to clarify. Daily methotrexate in place of weekly dosing is a well-known serious error.

Source: UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2024 Governing Medical Products, Pharmacists and Pharmaceutical Establishments, Art. 80

Question 9

A patient presents a valid prescription for a narcotic analgesic on which the physician has written 'repeat twice'. The first supply was dispensed last week. Under UAE federal law, may the pharmacist dispense a repeat?

  1. Yes, because the prescriber authorised the repeats
  2. No, narcotic prescriptions may not be repeated
  3. Yes, if the prescription is still within its validity
  4. Yes, but only once, whatever the prescriber wrote
أظهر الإجابة

B. No, narcotic prescriptions may not be repeated Article 37 of the narcotics law prohibits repeat dispensing of medical prescriptions for narcotics. A repeat written by the prescriber does not change this. The same article leaves the number of repeats allowed for psychotropic substances to a resolution, so repeats are possible only for listed psychotropics, not narcotics. The patient needs a new prescription for each narcotic supply. The prescription's validity period does not allow a repeat either.

Source: UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 30 of 2021 on Combating Narcotics and Psychotropic Substances, Art. 37(2)

Question 10

A new hospital employee received MMR vaccine yesterday and now needs a tuberculin skin test (TST). What does CDC advise?

  1. Do the TST today; MMR has no effect
  2. Repeat the MMR before the TST
  3. Delay the TST for at least 4 weeks
  4. Delay the TST for 1 year
أظهر الإجابة

C. Delay the TST for at least 4 weeks Measles-containing vaccines can temporarily suppress the tuberculin skin test response and cause a false-negative result. CDC advises that a TST can be given on the same day as MMR if needed; otherwise, once MMR has been given, the TST should be delayed for at least 4 weeks. The same applies to interferon-gamma release assays. MMR is a live vaccine, contraindicated in pregnancy and severe immunosuppression, and women should avoid pregnancy for 4 weeks after it.

Source: CDC Pink Book – Chapter 13: Measles

Question 11

Under current European and UK safety restrictions, an adult is prescribed domperidone for nausea and vomiting. What is the usual maximum treatment duration?

  1. One week
  2. Two weeks
  3. One month
  4. Three months
أظهر الإجابة

A. One week Because of the risk of serious cardiac effects, including QT prolongation and arrhythmias, European and UK regulators restricted domperidone to the relief of nausea and vomiting at the lowest effective dose. The maximum oral dose for adults is 30 mg in 24 hours, and treatment should not usually exceed one week. Longer courses of two weeks, a month or three months are outside these restrictions.

Source: MHRA Drug Safety Update – Domperidone: risks of cardiac side effects

Question 12

A woman 24 weeks pregnant asks for naproxen for back pain. What is the best advice?

  1. Safe at any stage of pregnancy if taken with food
  2. Safe if she uses it for less than 10 days in a row
  3. Avoid only during the first trimester of pregnancy
  4. Avoid from 20 weeks unless her doctor advises it
أظهر الإجابة

D. Avoid from 20 weeks unless her doctor advises it NSAIDs such as naproxen can harm the fetus when taken around 20 weeks of pregnancy or later, causing reduced amniotic fluid from fetal kidney effects and, later in pregnancy, premature closure of the ductus arteriosus. They should not be used from about 20 weeks unless a doctor advises it. Taking it with food or for a short course does not remove this risk. Paracetamol is the usual alternative for pain.

Source: MedlinePlus – Naproxen

Question 13

A man with itchy, peeling skin between his toes is diagnosed with athlete's foot and buys clotrimazole cream. How long should he continue it?

  1. Until the itching stops, then stop
  2. 1–2 weeks after it has cleared
  3. For exactly 3 days
  4. Only on days when symptoms are present
أظهر الإجابة

B. 1–2 weeks after it has cleared OTC antifungal creams or powders containing miconazole, clotrimazole, terbinafine or tolnaftate control athlete's foot. Patients should keep using the medicine for 1 to 2 weeks after the infection has cleared to prevent it from returning, and keep the feet clean and dry, especially between the toes. Stopping when itching stops or using it intermittently often leads to relapse.

Source: MedlinePlus Medical Encyclopedia – Athlete's foot

Question 14

A mother wants to put a camphor-menthol chest rub (VapoRub) inside her 18-month-old's nostrils for congestion. What should the pharmacist advise?

  1. Use a small amount inside each nostril
  2. Warm it in hot water for steam inhalation
  3. Mix it with honey and give it by mouth
  4. Do not use it in children under 2
أظهر الإجابة

D. Do not use it in children under 2 Camphor-menthol rubs are for external use only. The Drug Facts say not to use them by mouth, in nostrils, on wounds or damaged skin, or with tight bandages, and not to heat or microwave them or add them to hot water. They must not be used in children under 2 years, in whom camphor can cause serious toxicity. Saline drops and suction are safer for infants.

Source: DailyMed – VICKS VAPORUB ointment Drug Facts

Question 15

A woman with shingles asks whether she can infect her unvaccinated granddaughter, who has never had chickenpox. What is correct?

  1. The child can catch shingles
  2. Only adults can catch the virus
  3. Chickenpox is possible; cover it
  4. Shingles cannot spread to anyone
أظهر الإجابة

C. Chickenpox is possible; cover it People with shingles can spread varicella-zoster virus to people who have never had chickenpox or the vaccine; those people develop chickenpox, not shingles, since shingles cannot be caught from someone with shingles. Covering the rash lowers the risk, and contact with pregnant women without immunity and premature or low-birth-weight infants should be avoided until the blisters crust.

Source: CDC – About Shingles (Herpes Zoster)
