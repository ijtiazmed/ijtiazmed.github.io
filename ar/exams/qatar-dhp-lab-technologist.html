Question 1

A patient types as group O on forward grouping, but the plasma strongly agglutinates group O reagent red cells. The red cells do not react with anti-H lectin. Which red cells can the patient safely receive?

  1. Group AB red cells
  2. Bombay (Oh) red cells only
  3. Group O Rh-negative red cells
  4. Group O red cells after washing
أظهر الإجابة

B. Bombay (Oh) red cells only This is the Bombay phenotype (Oh): the red cells lack H, A and B antigens, and the plasma contains anti-A, anti-B and a strong, haemolytic anti-H. Group O cells carry the most H antigen, so they would cause an acute haemolytic reaction; group AB cells carry A and B antigens as well as H. Washing does not remove antigens. Only Bombay red cells (or the patient's own stored blood) are safe.

Source: Case report – Bombay phenotype and hemolytic transfusion reaction (PubMed Central, free full text)

Question 2

Why do many hospitals use a 'type and screen' approach, crossmatching blood only when it is actually needed?

  1. A crossmatch adds little after a negative screen
  2. The crossmatch detects ABO errors less well than the screen
  3. The screen also detects bacterial contamination
  4. Crossmatching is not allowed before surgery
أظهر الإجابة

A. A crossmatch adds little after a negative screen ABO/Rh typing plus an antibody screen detects almost all clinically significant incompatibility; adding a full crossmatch increases detection by only about 0.01%. So for patients with a negative screen, blood is typed and screened, and an immediate-spin or electronic crossmatch is done only when units are issued, saving reagents and units. The crossmatch is in fact a final ABO check. The antibody screen does not detect bacteria. Crossmatching before surgery is allowed.

Source: MSD Manual Professional – Blood Collection (Pretransfusion Testing)

Question 3

Where in the kidney are urinary casts formed?

  1. In the glomerulus (Bowman's space)
  2. In the proximal convoluted tubule only
  3. In the distal tubule and collecting duct
  4. In the renal pelvis and ureter
أظهر الإجابة

C. In the distal tubule and collecting duct Casts form in the distal nephron, in the distal convoluted tubule and collecting duct, where urine is most concentrated and acidic and flow is slow. Uromodulin protein gels there and traps any cells present. That is why cellular casts show that cells came from the kidney itself, not from the bladder or urethra. Casts are not formed in the glomerulus or lower urinary tract.

Source: WebPath (University of Utah) – Lab Medicine curriculum: Urinalysis

Question 4

An overnight 1-mg dexamethasone suppression test is ordered to screen for Cushing syndrome. When are the dexamethasone and the cortisol sample taken?

  1. Dexamethasone at noon, cortisol 1 hour later
  2. Dexamethasone at 8 AM, cortisol at 8 PM
  3. Dexamethasone at 11 PM, serum cortisol at 8 AM
  4. Cortisol first, dexamethasone the next day
أظهر الإجابة

C. Dexamethasone at 11 PM, serum cortisol at 8 AM In the overnight test, 1 mg of dexamethasone is taken orally at 11 PM to midnight and serum cortisol is measured at 8–9 AM the next morning. Normally dexamethasone suppresses ACTH and cortisol; patients with Cushing syndrome fail to suppress. Taking the sample the same evening or 1 hour after the dose does not allow suppression to be assessed. Measuring cortisol before the dose does not test suppression.

Source: MSD Manual Professional – Cushing Syndrome

Question 5

A breastfed newborn develops jaundice, poor feeding, liver damage and E. coli sepsis. Urine reducing substances are positive. Which enzyme is most likely deficient?

  1. Glucose-6-phosphatase
  2. Phenylalanine hydroxylase
  3. Branched-chain α-keto acid dehydrogenase
  4. Galactose-1-phosphate uridyltransferase
أظهر الإجابة

D. Galactose-1-phosphate uridyltransferase Classic galactosaemia (type I) is caused by variants in the GALT gene that almost abolish galactose-1-phosphate uridyltransferase activity. When the infant drinks breast milk or formula containing lactose, galactose metabolites build up, causing jaundice, liver damage, bleeding, cataracts and life-threatening bacterial sepsis. Phenylalanine hydroxylase deficiency causes PKU, branched-chain α-keto acid dehydrogenase deficiency causes maple syrup urine disease, and glucose-6-phosphatase deficiency causes von Gierke disease.

Source: MedlinePlus Genetics – Galactosemia

Question 6

Which coagulation factors depend on vitamin K for their synthesis in the liver?

  1. II, VII, IX and X
  2. I, V, VIII and XIII
  3. VIII, IX, XI and XII
  4. III, IV, V and VI
أظهر الإجابة

A. II, VII, IX and X Vitamin K controls the liver's formation of factors II (prothrombin), VII, IX and X, and the anticoagulant proteins C and S, by enabling gamma-carboxylation. Warfarin works by blocking vitamin K recycling, which lowers these factors. Factors I, V, VIII and XIII do not need vitamin K; factor VIII is made mainly outside hepatocytes.

Source: MSD Manual Professional – Vitamin K Deficiency

Question 7

A woman at 34 weeks of pregnancy has mild leg swelling, and her D-dimer is raised. Compression ultrasound is normal. Why is the raised D-dimer not diagnostic?

  1. A raised D-dimer excludes DVT
  2. D-dimer is very specific for DVT
  3. D-dimer is falsely low in pregnancy
  4. Pregnancy itself raises D-dimer
أظهر الإجابة

D. Pregnancy itself raises D-dimer D-dimer is highly sensitive but nonspecific. It rises in pregnancy and also in liver disease, trauma, inflammation, recent surgery, cancer and with age, so a positive result cannot diagnose DVT by itself. Its value is in ruling out thrombosis when the result is negative and the clinical probability is low.

Source: MSD Manual Professional – Deep Venous Thrombosis (DVT)

Question 8

In the reverse syphilis screening algorithm, a treponemal EIA is reactive but the RPR is nonreactive. What is the next test?

  1. Report the patient as syphilis cured
  2. A second treponemal test such as TP-PA
  3. An HIV Western blot as confirmation
  4. Repeat the RPR test again after 1 year
أظهر الإجابة

B. A second treponemal test such as TP-PA In reverse-sequence screening, a reactive treponemal immunoassay is followed by a nontreponemal test (RPR). If the RPR is nonreactive, a second treponemal test on a different platform or antigen, commonly TP-PA, resolves the discordance. A reactive TP-PA fits past or current syphilis, while a nonreactive one suggests a false-positive screen or very early infection.

Source: APHL/CDC – Suggested Reporting Language for Syphilis Serological Testing (2020)

Question 9

A child has haemolytic anaemia with schistocytes, thrombocytopenia and acute kidney failure without preceding diarrhoea. ADAMTS13 activity is normal. Overactivity of which system causes this disorder?

  1. Fibrinolytic (plasmin) system
  2. Kinin–kallikrein system
  3. Complement (alternative pathway)
  4. Classical antibody response
أظهر الإجابة

C. Complement (alternative pathway) Atypical haemolytic uraemic syndrome has three main features: haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia and kidney failure. It is usually caused by variants in genes that regulate the complement system, such as factor H, so the alternative pathway is activated without control and damages small vessels. Normal ADAMTS13 activity excludes TTP, and the absence of bloody diarrhoea makes Shiga toxin HUS unlikely. Complement inhibitors are used to treat it.

Source: MedlinePlus Genetics – Atypical Hemolytic-Uremic Syndrome

Question 10

Immunohistochemistry on formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue gives weak staining. Why is heat-induced epitope retrieval used before applying the primary antibody?

  1. To counterstain the nuclei
  2. To remove the paraffin wax
  3. To block endogenous peroxidase
  4. To break formalin cross-links
أظهر الإجابة

D. To break formalin cross-links Formalin fixation forms methylene cross-links between proteins that mask antigen sites (epitopes). Heating sections in buffers such as citrate or EDTA breaks these cross-links and unmasks the epitope, so the antibody can bind. Paraffin is removed earlier with xylene. Endogenous peroxidase is blocked with hydrogen peroxide. Nuclei are counterstained with haematoxylin at the end.

Source: Leica Biosystems – Technical Brief of Heat Induced Epitope Retrieval

Question 11

A diabetic patient in ketoacidosis has a black necrotic lesion of the nasal turbinate. Tissue shows broad, ribbon-like hyphae with few or no septa, branching at about 90°. Which fungus is most likely?

  1. Aspergillus fumigatus
  2. Cryptococcus neoformans
  3. Candida albicans
  4. Rhizopus (Mucorales)
أظهر الإجابة

D. Rhizopus (Mucorales) Mucorales (Mucor, Rhizopus) cause rhino-orbital-cerebral mucormycosis, classically in diabetic ketoacidosis. In tissue they form broad, ribbon-like, pauci-septate hyphae with irregular widths and right-angle branching. Aspergillus forms narrow septate hyphae with acute-angle (about 45°) branching. Candida appears as yeasts and pseudohyphae. Cryptococcus is an encapsulated budding yeast that is seen with India ink or mucicarmine.

Source: MSD Manual Professional – Mucormycosis

Question 12

A patient with suspected chronic brucellosis has negative blood cultures. Which culture specimen has the highest sensitivity?

  1. Bone marrow aspirate
  2. Early-morning urine
  3. Throat swab in transport medium
  4. Stool in Cary-Blair medium
أظهر الإجابة

A. Bone marrow aspirate Brucella lives inside macrophages of the reticuloendothelial system, so bone marrow culture is more sensitive than blood culture and is often considered the gold standard, especially in chronic disease or after antibiotics. The laboratory must be warned, because Brucella is a leading cause of laboratory-acquired infection, and cultures may need prolonged incubation. Urine, throat swabs and stool are poor specimens for Brucella.

Source: MSD Manual Professional – Brucellosis

Question 13

Which chemical is commonly used as a high-level disinfectant for heat-sensitive equipment such as endoscopes?

  1. Quaternary ammonium compound
  2. 70% ethanol
  3. Chlorhexidine
  4. Glutaraldehyde
أظهر الإجابة

D. Glutaraldehyde Glutaraldehyde is a high-level disinfectant (sporicidal with long enough contact) widely used for heat-sensitive equipment such as endoscopes; it needs good ventilation because its vapour irritates the skin and airways. 70% ethanol is an intermediate-level disinfectant that does not kill spores. Chlorhexidine is a skin antiseptic. Quaternary ammonium compounds are low-level disinfectants for surfaces.

Source: CDC – Disinfection and Sterilization Guideline: Chemical Disinfectants

Question 14

Why do laboratories use Westgard multirule QC instead of rejecting every run with a control outside 2 SD?

  1. Fewer false rejections, good error detection
  2. It allows some control runs to be skipped
  3. It replaces the need for regular calibration
  4. It keeps all results within 1 SD of the mean
أظهر الإجابة

A. Fewer false rejections, good error detection With two controls and 2 SD limits alone, many runs would be rejected by chance (false rejections). Multirule QC combines rules with very low false-rejection rates (such as 1-3s, 2-2s, R-4s and 4-1s) so that real random and systematic errors are detected while false rejections stay low; 1-2s is used as a warning. It does not replace running controls or calibration, and it does not change the spread of results.

Source: Westgard QC – Westgard Rules and Multirule QC

Question 15

A patient has died. A neighbour of the family calls the laboratory to ask whether the patient's HIV test was positive. What should the laboratory specialist do?

  1. Tell the neighbour, as the patient has died
  2. Refuse, as confidentiality continues after death
  3. Tell the neighbour if the result was negative
  4. Post the result on the ward notice board
أظهر الإجابة

B. Refuse, as confidentiality continues after death The duty of confidentiality does not end when the patient dies. Results may be disclosed only with valid consent, to those legally entitled, or in exceptional cases where disclosure is needed to meet an overriding ethical obligation, and then only the minimum information to the minimum recipients. A neighbour has no such right, so the specialist should decline politely and not confirm or deny any result.

Source: WMA International Code of Medical Ethics (2022)
